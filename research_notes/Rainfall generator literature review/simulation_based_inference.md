# Simulation-Based Inference (SBI) and Likelihood-Free Calibration for Rainfall Generators, Weather Generators and Stochastic Hydrology / Urban Drainage (c. 2010-2026)

Scope note: about 20 searches and fetches (2026-10-01). Full texts were read for Northrop (2023/24 Annual Review) and Groenke et al. (2026, JGR-MLC). Other papers are cited from their abstracts, publisher pages or secondary descriptions; each place this applies is marked. **Overall the literature is thin.** Only a few papers apply ABC to Poisson-cluster rainfall models, and I found only one peer-reviewed paper applying *neural* SBI to a precipitation or weather generator, and that one works at daily resolution. I found no paper applying SBI to a deep generative rainfall model, and none combining SBI with RL control of water systems.

## 1. ABC / likelihood-free calibration of Poisson-cluster (Bartlett-Lewis, Neyman-Scott) and other stochastic rainfall models

### Takeaway
Only one group has used ABC to fit Poisson-cluster rainfall models: Aryal & Jones (2020, temporal Bartlett-Lewis on 6-min gauge data; 2021, spatial-temporal model on radar data). Their 2020 paper describes itself as the first use of ABC to fit rainfall models, and it reports a better fit than the standard generalised method of moments (GMM/GMOM). The main gain is freedom to use summary statistics that have no closed-form expectation, such as wet/dry spell length distributions, and freedom to relax assumptions that were only made to keep the model analytically tractable. Everyone else still fits these models by GMOM, with some MCMC-Bayesian fits (not likelihood-free) for NSRP.

### Cited Findings
- Aryal, N.R. & Jones, O.D. (2020). "Fitting the Bartlett-Lewis rainfall model using Approximate Bayesian Computation." *Mathematics and Computers in Simulation* 175:153-163. The BL likelihood is intractable and the model has historically been fitted by GMM. GMM needs summary statistics with closed-form means, while ABC "places no restrictions on the summary statistics". The authors report that ABC "gives a better fit than GMM" and say this "appears to be the first time ABC has been used to fit rainfall models" (abstract). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378475419303313); [ORCA Cardiff record](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/126617/); [RePEc (authors/volume)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/matcom/v175y2020icp153-163.html)
- Summary statistics in Aryal & Jones (2020) were computed on rainfall aggregated to 6-minute and hourly intervals: mean, standard deviation, lag-1 and lag-2 autocorrelation, probability of no rain, mean and SD of wet and dry period lengths, and the total number of wet and dry periods. Data: 6-min totals from Victoria, Australia. The sampler used ABC with a random-walk Metropolis-Hastings proposal step and a reparameterisation that reduces posterior dependence between parameters. — [Northrop 2023, Annu. Rev. Stat. Appl., §6.1.1 and §6.2 (UCL open-access PDF)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10174912/1/StochasticModelsOfRainfall.pdf); statistics list also in the [ResearchGate preprint snippet](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326552309_Fitting_the_Bartlett-Lewis_rainfall_model_using_Approximate_Bayesian_Computation)
- Aryal, N.R. & Jones, O.D. (2021). "Spatial-temporal rainfall models based on Poisson cluster processes." *Stochastic Environmental Research and Risk Assessment* 35(12):2629-2643. Uses ABC to fit extended versions of Northrop's (1998) spatial-temporal model to 1 km² radar data from Melbourne. The extensions relax assumptions that had been made only so that model properties could be derived; for example, cell intensity now decays from the cell centre to its edge. Velocity and shape parameters were fixed from the data to save computation. Summary statistics included wet/dry *area* distributions in radar images. Some of the extensions improved the fit. — [Northrop 2023 review, §6.2](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10174912/1/StochasticModelsOfRainfall.pdf) (secondary; I did not read the 2021 paper itself)
- Northrop's review describes ABC as "a Bayesian implementation of GMM": it accepts θ when the GMOM quadratic form Q(θ;T) falls below a threshold ε. Its verdict is that ABC lets us "fit models that we would really like to fit using the properties that we would really like to use", at the cost of more computation, the need to choose ε and tuning parameters, and the same starting-value problem as GMOM. It points to Fearnhead & Prangle (2012) semi-automatic ABC as a way to choose summary statistics from data. — [Northrop, "Stochastic models of rainfall", Annu. Rev. Stat. Appl. 11 (2024), doi 10.1146/annurev-statistics-040622-023838](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-statistics-040622-023838)
- Background on standard GMOM, which is the "method of moments" comparison point. The objective is Q(θ;T) = (T−µ(θ))'W(T−µ(θ)). Jesus & Chandler (2011) showed that the optimal W is S⁻¹, but W = diag(Ŝ) performs comparably and beats the identity matrix "which has been a common choice in the hydrological literature". Kim & Olivera (2012) give recommendations on how to weight statistics for different applications. Statistics should have algebraic expectations under the model. Extremes are typically *not* used in fitting and are checked by simulation afterwards. A full likelihood approach is argued to "place undue emphasis on features that these models are not intended to reproduce". — [Northrop review §6, §6.1](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10174912/1/StochasticModelsOfRainfall.pdf)
- NSRP Bayesian (MCMC, not likelihood-free) estimation: Nizeyimana et al. (2024), "Bayesian Estimation of Neyman-Scott Rectangular Pulse Model Parameters in Comparison with Other Parameter Estimation Methods," *Water* 16(17):2515. They report that method-of-moments estimates were sensitive to initial values, optimiser and aggregation scale. Slice sampling within Gibbs gave stable, unique estimates and "outperform[ed] other estimation methods". — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/17/2515) (from abstract)
- I found no ABC or neural-SBI fitting of Neyman-Scott (NSRP) rainfall models. Current NSRP tooling (NEOPRENE, IH Cantabria) is calibrated to observed statistics at several aggregation levels, i.e. moment-type fitting. — [NEOPRENE GitHub](https://github.com/IHCantabria/NEOPRENE)
- A related use of ABC for rainfall *extremes*: Erhardt, R.J. & Smith, R.L. (2012), "Approximate Bayesian computing for spatial extremes," *Computational Statistics & Data Analysis* 56(6):1468-1481. It uses ABC for max-stable process models, whose joint likelihood is intractable. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167947311004245)

### Inferences
- ABC for rainfall generators is still a niche approach, essentially one group (Aryal & Jones), and there are no sub-hourly ABC studies in Europe. A 5-minute point-rainfall study like ours would be adding to a very small body of work if it engaged with this framing.
- The ABC summary statistics (wet/dry spell statistics, autocorrelation, probability of zero, multi-aggregation moments) are almost the same as the statistics we use as *evaluation* gates (Gate A). In the SBI literature the statistics are fitting targets; for us they are held-out diagnostics. This difference is worth stating explicitly when comparing approaches.

### Gaps
- I could not access the full text of Aryal & Jones (2020): the publisher and ORCA returned 403. The quantitative size of the "better than GMM" improvement, and whether extremes were assessed, are therefore unverified.
- No ABC work on Neyman-Scott, on DRIP-type models, or on HMM-based rainfall models was found.

## 2. Neural SBI (NPE/NLE/NRE, sbi package, BayesFlow) for rainfall, weather generators, hydrological, flood and urban drainage models

### Takeaway
Neural SBI has only just reached this field. The clearest precipitation example is Groenke et al. (2026, JGR: Machine Learning and Computation). They use sequential NPE (SNPE-C/APT, `sbi` package, masked autoregressive flows) on a **daily** GAMLSS temperature and precipitation generator. The aim is not historical calibration but **"climatology matching"**: finding generator parameters that reproduce *perturbed* target summary statistics for scenario-neutral climate stress tests. In hydrology, Hull et al. (2024, HESS) apply neural SBI to a ParFlow-CLM watershed model using an LSTM emulator. Urban-drainage and SWMM calibration still uses classical ABC (Chowdhury et al. 2022) or Bayesian optimisation and surrogates. I found no neural SBI for SWMM and no sub-daily rainfall generator calibrated with neural SBI.

### Cited Findings
- **Groenke, B., Wessel, J., Miersch, P., Klein, N. & Zscheischler, J. (2026).** "Stochastic weather generation for scenario-neutral impact assessments using simulation-based inference." *JGR: Machine Learning and Computation* 3, e2025JH000902 (received 17 Jul 2025, accepted 31 Mar 2026). — [Wiley](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2025JH000902); [PIK PDF](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/rest/items/item_34299_1/component/file_34300/content); [ESSOAr preprint](https://essopenarchive.org/doi/full/10.22541/essoar.175242145.59540368)
  - The generator is GAMLSS-based and simulates daily mean, minimum and maximum temperature and daily precipitation over multi-decadal periods. It is calibrated on German Weather Service (DWD) station data, with Potsdam as the case study. — [PIK PDF, abstract](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/rest/items/item_34299_1/component/file_34300/content)
  - Method: SNPE-C/APT (Greenberg et al. 2019) from the `sbi` package with default masked autoregressive flows. Each round draws 1,000 parameter samples, and each sample simulates a 50-year series from which summary statistics are computed. One run takes "roughly 10-30 min". — [PIK PDF §3.5](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/rest/items/item_34299_1/component/file_34300/content)
  - The paper justifies moving beyond ABC with three limitations taken from Papamakarios & Murray (2016). ABC gives samples rather than a density. The samples come from the ε-approximate posterior, not the true posterior, and depend on the distance metric. And the required simulations "become intractably large" as ε→0 and parameter dimension grows. — [PIK PDF](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/rest/items/item_34299_1/component/file_34300/content)
  - Targets are summary statistics or climate indices (long-term averages, percentiles, indices such as CDD and precipitation frequency). The paper reports cases where a target is anomalous under the prior predictive, a form of prior/target misspecification ("pathological behavior" for CDD and precipitation occurrence). Proposed remedies are prior elicitation methods or the misspecification-robust Bayes of Knoblauch et al. (2022). On one station, the SBI posterior partly removed a historical bias in precipitation occurrence, which suggests SBI "could also be used to improve weather generator performance over the historical period". — [PIK PDF, discussion and appendices](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/rest/items/item_34299_1/component/file_34300/content)
  - The authors call it "one of the first applications of SBI to the domain of weather and climate simulation". They list extreme-rainfall storylines feeding hydrological models as future work. — [PIK PDF](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/rest/items/item_34299_1/component/file_34300/content)
  - The code is released as `wxsbi` (numpyro probabilistic programming plus `sbi`) and is labelled experimental. — [GitHub bgroenks96/wxsbi](https://github.com/bgroenks96/wxsbi)
- **Hull et al. (2024).** "Simulation-based inference for parameter estimation of complex watershed simulators," *HESS* 28:4685. Neural conditional density estimation is used to infer Manning's roughness and hydraulic conductivity in ParFlow-CLM for a snowmelt-dominated catchment. An LSTM emulator stands in for the simulator. There are four experiments: best case, a "tough case" where emulator bias appears, a "boosted" case with an ensemble of surrogates, and a "weighted" case using KGE-based informal likelihood weighting. The weighted case gives accurate estimates despite simulator misspecification. — [HESS](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/28/4685/2024/); [PNNL summary](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/simulation-based-inference-parameter-estimation-complex-watershed-simulators)
- **Classical ABC in hydrology**: Vrugt & Sadegh (2013), "Toward diagnostic model calibration and evaluation: Approximate Bayesian computation," *WRR*; Sadegh & Vrugt (2014), "Approximate Bayesian Computation using Markov Chain Monte Carlo simulation: DREAM(ABC)," *WRR*; Sadegh, Vrugt, Xu & Volpi (2015), "The stationarity paradigm revisited: Hypothesis testing using diagnostics, summary metrics, and DREAM(ABC)," *WRR*. These papers replace residual likelihoods with diagnostic summary metrics such as flow-duration-curve signatures. — [search listing and ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260724623_Bridging_the_gap_between_GLUE_and_formal_statistical_approaches_Approximate_Bayesian_computation) (titles and years from the search summary; full texts not read)
- Andraos, C. (2024). "Breaking Uncertainty Barriers: Approximate Bayesian Computation Advances in Rainfall-Runoff Modeling," *Water* 16(23):3499. Applies ABC to the MEDOR conceptual model for the Nahr Ibrahim catchment in Lebanon. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/23/3499)
- ABC applied to climate-impact streamflow statistics: "How to assess climate change impact models: uncertainty analysis of streamflow statistics via approximate Bayesian computation (ABC)," *Hydrological Sciences Journal* (2023). — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02626667.2023.2231437) (title only; authors not verified)
- **Urban drainage**: Chowdhury, A. et al. (2022). "Automatic model calibration of combined hydrologic, hydraulic and stormwater quality models using approximate Bayesian computation," *Water Science & Technology* 86(2):321-332, doi 10.2166/wst.2022.207. ABC is used to calibrate combined runoff and TSS (pollutograph) models over several events in three catchments, and the observations fall within the 95% intervals. — [IWA (open access)](https://iwaponline.com/wst/article/86/2/321/89618/Automatic-model-calibration-of-combined-hydrologic); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35906910/) (abstract only; I could not confirm whether the hydraulic engine is SWMM)
- Other SWMM calibration uses Bayesian optimisation (Bayes_Opt-SWMM, *Environmental Modelling & Software* 2024) or PCE surrogates, not SBI. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S136481522400183X); [arXiv 1709.03283](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.03283)
- General tooling: BayesFlow provides amortised SBI with neural networks and can detect model misspecification. I found no BayesFlow application to rainfall or rainfall-runoff models. — [arXiv 2306.16015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16015)
- Framing reference: Cranmer, K., Brehmer, J. & Louppe, G. (2020), "The frontier of simulation-based inference," *PNAS* 117(48):30055-30062. — [arXiv 1911.01429](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01429) (journal details from my own knowledge of the paper, not re-verified in this session)

### Inferences
- In the one weather-generator application, SBI is used for *steering* a parametric generator: it finds the θ that reproduces a target climate, real or perturbed. It is not used to replace the generator. That is the capability our diffusion model lacks. We can condition on HMM state, but we cannot ask for "a 10-year record whose 5-min annual-maximum distribution is shifted by +15%".
- Neural SBI has replaced ABC in the newest work mainly because it is cheaper per simulation and amortised. The summary-statistic choice problem has not gone away, and the misspecification problem is now explicitly acknowledged (Groenke; Hull).

### Gaps
- No neural SBI (NPE/NLE/NRE) application to sub-daily rainfall generators (BL/NSRP/HMM) was found. This could be a genuine gap or missed by search.
- No neural SBI for SWMM or urban drainage calibration was found.

## 3. SBI with deep generative models as simulators / deep generative emulators / differentiable simulators

### Takeaway
I found nothing that does SBI *on* a deep generative rainfall model (diffusion, GAN or VAE) as the simulator. Such models have no low-dimensional, physically meaningful θ to infer, so standard SBI does not apply directly. The related work runs the other way: neural networks as *emulators inside* SBI (Hull et al.'s LSTM surrogate; "emulator networks"), and generative models as *priors* in Bayesian inverse problems.

### Cited Findings
- Hull et al. (2024) used an LSTM trained on ParFlow runs as a fast emulator inside SBI and showed that emulator bias causes misspecification (the "tough case"), which is reduced by ensembles or weighting. — [HESS](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/28/4685/2024/)
- Lueckmann et al., "Likelihood-free inference with emulator networks" (2018) learn a probabilistic emulator of the simulator, which gives a tractable synthetic likelihood. — [arXiv 1805.09294](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09294)
- Diffusion models are appearing as *priors* for precipitation inverse problems, e.g. "Bayesian Rain Field Reconstruction using Commercial Microwave Links and Diffusion Model Priors" (arXiv 2605.05520). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05520) (title only; not read)
- Groenke et al. describe their GAMLSS generator as a probabilistic program (numpyro). A differentiable, explicitly parameterised generator is what makes SBI convenient there. — [wxsbi](https://github.com/bgroenks96/wxsbi)

### Inferences
- Our diffusion model has class labels (HMM states) as its only explicit, low-dimensional control. A possible bridge to the SBI literature is to treat the label, or a small set of conditioning variables such as a state-transition plan or an intensity scale, as θ and use NPE to infer which conditioning reproduces target statistics. This would be climatology matching (Groenke) applied to a deep generator. I found no precedent for it.
- Conversely, the diffusion model could serve as a cheap *emulator of observed rainfall*, used to train SBI for parametric models. I found no precedent for this either.

### Gaps
- No literature found on SBI with diffusion, GAN or VAE rainfall generators as simulators, or on "deep generative models as emulators" for *stochastic rainfall* specifically.

## 4. Advantages and limitations of SBI relative to our direct-data diffusion approach

### Takeaway
SBI-calibrated parametric generators offer interpretable parameters with posterior uncertainty, the ability to put in non-closed-form statistics, and principled steering to perturbed or future climates. The methods depend on the chosen summary statistics, fit only what those statistics encode, and suffer under model misspecification. A data-driven diffusion model avoids the need to choose statistics and a parametric structure, but it gives no parameter uncertainty and no direct handle on climate-change perturbation.

### Cited Findings
- Advantages (ABC for rainfall): unrealistic simplifying assumptions can be relaxed without deriving model properties, and summary statistics can be chosen freely. — [Northrop review §6.2](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10174912/1/StochasticModelsOfRainfall.pdf)
- Advantages (neural SBI): a parametric posterior density, no distance threshold, adaptive simulation efficiency, and amortisation, meaning the trained network can be reused for new data. — [Hull et al. 2024](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/28/4685/2024/); [Groenke et al. 2026](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/rest/items/item_34299_1/component/file_34300/content)
- Climate-change extrapolation: SBI generates series "under arbitrary climate conditions as defined by any relevant set of summary statistics" for scenario-neutral stress-testing. — [Groenke et al. 2026](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2025JH000902)
- Regionalisation and nonstationarity in Poisson-cluster models is currently handled by GMOM extensions, not SBI. Examples are local GMOM with covariates (Kaczmarska et al. 2015) and kriging parameters across US sites (LetItRain, Kim et al. 2017). — [Northrop review](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10174912/1/StochasticModelsOfRainfall.pdf)
- Limitations: results depend on the choice of summary statistics and ε. Parameters fit only the statistics used, so validation must use statistics not used for fitting, and extremes are usually checked only by simulation. — [Northrop review §6.1.1, §6.2](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10174912/1/StochasticModelsOfRainfall.pdf)
- Limitations: prior or target misspecification, where the target statistics are anomalous under the prior predictive. — [Groenke et al. 2026](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/rest/items/item_34299_1/component/file_34300/content); simulator or emulator misspecification — [Hull et al. 2024](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/28/4685/2024/)

### Inferences
- **Positioning our work.** Our diffusion model is a nonparametric density estimator over 5-min sequences. There is no θ, so "calibration" amounts to training by maximum likelihood (a denoising score-matching objective) on raw sequences instead of matching summary statistics. This is the same move the GMOM-vs-likelihood debate warns about: a full-likelihood fit spends capacity on features one may not care about (Northrop/Jesus & Chandler). It is also the reason our tails can be under-fitted, since extremes carry only a small share of the loss. The SBI/GMOM tradition makes the opposite trade-off.
- **What we could borrow:** (a) treat the SBI-style summary-statistic vector (multi-aggregation moments, wet/dry spell statistics, autocorrelation, extremes) as an explicit acceptance test, which our Gate A already approximates; (b) a fitted Bartlett-Lewis or NSRP model calibrated by GMOM or ABC on the same Astlingen 2000-2007 data would be a natural *parametric baseline* to compare against the diffusion model on Gate A and on downstream RL performance; (c) climatology matching could become a future-work direction, steering the generator's conditioning toward perturbed extremes for stress-testing RL controllers.
- **Interpretability and uncertainty:** with a single trained diffusion checkpoint we get aleatoric variability but no epistemic uncertainty about the rainfall regime. A thesis should acknowledge this as a difference from Bayesian or SBI-calibrated generators.

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly compares SBI-calibrated parametric generators with deep generative rainfall models on the same data.

## 5. SBI combined with RL or control for water systems

### Takeaway
I found no literature combining SBI or likelihood-free calibration with reinforcement learning or real-time control for water, stormwater or sewer systems. RL for stormwater control exists (often SWMM-based), but it trains on observed or design storms, not on SBI-calibrated stochastic rainfall.

### Cited Findings
- Deep RL for real-time stormwater control in SWMM5 under uncertain data: Saliba et al. (2020), "Deep Reinforcement Learning with Uncertain Data for Real-Time Stormwater System Control and Flood Mitigation," *Water* 12(11):3222. They report a 70.5% average reduction in flooding volume versus passive systems. — [MDPI](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/12/11/3222/htm) (author name from my own knowledge, not verified in this session; the figure is from the search summary)
- Wang et al. (2020), "Flooding Control by Reinforcement Learning," ISCRAM. — [ISCRAM PDF](https://idl.iscram.org/files/chengwang/2020/2202_ChengWang_etal2020.pdf)
- Deep Q-Network reservoir operation for flood mitigation: *Water* 17(22):3226 (2025). — [MDPI doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/w17223226)

### Inferences
- Using a stochastic rainfall generator, whether SBI-calibrated or deep-generative, as a training-episode source for RL sewer control looks like a novel combination. The closest conceptual link is Groenke et al.'s proposal to use SBI-steered generators to stress-test impact models under changed extremes.

### Gaps
- My searches were not exhaustive on RL. A dedicated search on "synthetic rainfall for RL training in urban drainage" belongs to a different research thread.
