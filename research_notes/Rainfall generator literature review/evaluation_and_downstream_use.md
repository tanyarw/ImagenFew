## Q1. What do rainfall-generator papers (classical and ML) report? Are there accepted protocols? Are noise floors, C2ST, precision/recall, memorisation or TSTR used?

### Takeaway
Classical sub-daily generator papers use a fairly stable set of statistics: moments across aggregation scales, lag-1 autocorrelation, dry proportion, wet/dry spells, extreme quantiles or annual maxima, and IDF reproduction. There is no formal benchmark or standard protocol. I found no rainfall-generator paper that uses a real-vs-real noise floor, C2ST, PRDC/alpha-precision, a memorisation check or TSTR. Those ideas come from ML generative evaluation. ML precipitation papers (mostly downscaling and nowcasting, not point time-series generation) use spatial and ensemble metrics such as CRPS, rank histograms, power spectra and extreme quantiles.

### Cited Findings
- **Standard classical statistics.** A comparison of daily-to-sub-daily disaggregation methods (method of fragments, multiplicative cascades, randomised Bartlett-Lewis) judged them on "standard validation statistics, rainfall variability and intermittency, within-day wet spells, and extreme rainfall percentiles". All methods reproduced the mean, variance, lag-1 autocorrelation and dry proportions, but only the method of fragments matched observed IFD behaviour at the hourly scale. Cascades mis-estimated extremes and were "not robust for observed wet and dry spells" (snippet). [V] — [A comparison of alternatives for daily to sub-daily rainfall disaggregation, J. Hydrology 2012 (Pui, Sharma, Mehrotra, Sivakumar, Jeremiah — authors [M])](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169412007202)
- **Bartlett-Lewis lineage.** Kaczmarska/Onof-type work fits and validates on the mean, coefficient of variation, lag-1 autocorrelation and skewness of depths at several aggregation levels (snippet). [V] — [Modelling rainfall with a Bartlett–Lewis process: new developments, HESS 24, 2791, 2020 (Onof & Wang)](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/24/2791/2020/)
- **Recent sub-hourly (10-min) generator, Germany.** Evaluated on the mean and variance at monthly and annual scale, transition probabilities, ACF, proportion wet, frequency of exceedances of 2-15 mm/10 min, quantiles from 0.99 to 0.99999, and annual maxima. No urban hydrological or sewer model was run; the authors list this as future work: "A downstream application of simulated data in urban hydrological models would also be beneficial." [V] — [Vorobevskii, Park, Kim, Barfus, Kronenberg (2024), Simulating sub-hourly rainfall data for current and future periods using two statistical disaggregation models: case studies from Germany and South Korea, HESS 28, 391](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/28/391/2024/)
- **Across-scale reproduction as a goal.** One stochastic model is explicitly designed to "reproduce important rainfall properties across the timescales from several minutes to a decade" (title). Authors and year not verified; likely Park et al. 2021 [M]. — [J. Hydrology article S0022169420306107](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169420306107)
- **Software generator aimed at urban use.** STORAGE (STOchastic RAinfall GEnerator) produces long, high-resolution series. A follow-up Water Research paper tested it in urban flood analysis (see Q4). [V title] — [STORAGE, Hydrology 8(2):76, 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5338/8/2/76)
- **Multi-model comparison for 5-min urban series.** One study compared parametric stochastic, non-parametric resampling and stochastic-downscaling generators that produce 5-minute continuous series for sizing combined sewer systems (snippet). Identity not confirmed; probably the J. Hydrology 2017 "Short time step continuous rainfall modeling and simulation of extreme events" or a related paper. — [J. Hydrology S0022169417304390](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169417304390)
- **ML precipitation work is mostly spatial downscaling and nowcasting.** A 2025 intercomparison benchmarks GANs, diffusion and flow matching for fine-scale downscaling on "structure, extremes, and climate change signals". It reports that diffusion and flow matching give higher fidelity and better-calibrated ensembles than GANs. [V] — [An intercomparison of generative ML methods for downscaling precipitation at fine spatial scales, arXiv 2512.13987](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.13987). See also [Addison et al. 2024, ML emulation of precipitation from km-scale UK climate simulations using a diffusion model, arXiv 2407.14158](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.14158) [V title; authors M] and [Harris et al. 2022, A generative deep learning approach to stochastic downscaling of precipitation forecasts, JAMES](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2022MS003120) [V].
- **Rainfall downscaling GAN.** RainScaleGAN (2025) is judged on whether the generated precipitation distribution mirrors ground-truth statistics and spatial patterns. [V] — [RainScaleGAN, AIES 4(3), 2025](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/aies/4/3/AIES-D-24-0074.1.xml)
- **GAN for sewer time series.** A Bayesian GAN generated synthetic time series to augment combined-sewer flow prediction. This is a TSTR-style utility test, but on sewer flow rather than rainfall. [V title] — [A Bayesian GAN to Generate Synthetic Time-Series Data, Application in Combined Sewer Flow Prediction, arXiv 2301.13733](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13733)

### Inferences
- The project's rainfall-statistics block (water balance, dry fraction, spells, storm stats, IDF/Gumbel, ACF and variance across scales, seasonal and diurnal cycles) matches or exceeds the classical standard. The storm-duration survival curve and variance-across-scales are less common than the usual mean/var/lag-1/p0 set.
- Classical papers usually compare one generator realisation against one observed record without a sampling-variability yardstick. Some report ensembles of many synthetic series compared with observations (see the CSO snippet in Q4). A **held-out real-vs-real noise floor** is therefore a genuine methodological addition: it tells the reader which mismatches are larger than natural interannual variability.
- C2ST, PRDC, memorisation and TSTR appear not to be used in point-rainfall generation work, in either classical or deep-learning papers. This is a likely point of novelty. It should be stated cautiously ("we found no prior use"), because my search was limited.
- Deep generative models for **continuous multi-year point rainfall at 5-min resolution** appear rare. Most ML precipitation work is spatial (radar fields, downscaling). This supports the project's niche, though a dedicated search is still needed to rule out recent GAN/VAE/diffusion point-series generators (e.g. 2023-2026 WRR/HESS papers).

### Gaps
- I did not locate a formal community protocol or benchmark dataset for sub-daily generators. None appears to exist, but this is not confirmed.
- I did not find any deep-learning point-rainfall time-series generator paper (GAN/VAE/diffusion producing a multi-year gauge series) in this session. The searches returned nowcasting and downscaling work. This needs a dedicated search.
- Authors for the 2012 disaggregation comparison and the "minutes to a decade" model were not verified (publisher pages blocked).

## Q2. Generic time-series generative evaluation (TimeGAN scores, Context-FID, PRDC, alpha-precision) and known pitfalls

### Takeaway
The de facto ML protocol is TimeGAN's discriminative score (a post-hoc GRU classifier, ideally near 0.5 accuracy) and predictive score (TSTR with a GRU, MAE on real data), plus Context-FID, often with t-SNE/PCA plots. Benchmarks such as TSGBench criticise these measures as unstable (they depend on retraining random networks) and as ambiguously defined. Sample-level metrics (PRDC; alpha-precision, beta-recall, authenticity) add fidelity/diversity separation and a memorisation test.

### Cited Findings
- Common metrics are the predictive score (PS), discriminative score (DS) and Context-FID. DS trains a 2-layer GRU/LSTM classifier to separate real from generated series, with about 0.5 accuracy being ideal. PS trains a GRU/LSTM predictor on synthetic data and reports MAE on real data, which is TSTR. Context-FID replaces Inception with TS2Vec embeddings. [V] — [Diffusion-TS (Yuan & Qiao, ICLR 2024), arXiv 2403.01742](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01742)
- **Pitfall.** "DS and PS can yield unreliable evaluations because they rely on the TSTR scheme necessitating post-hoc model training, and the inherent randomness in deep models such as weight initialization can cause inconsistent evaluations" (search snippet, from a 2025 paper listed in the same results). [V snippet] — [DiM-TS, arXiv 2511.18312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18312) and/or [arXiv 2505.05020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.05020). The exact source sentence was not pinned down.
- **TSGBench** (Ang, Huang, Bao, Tung, Huang; VLDB 2024) uses 12 measures (vanilla, distance-based and visual) on 10 datasets. It flags three problems: limited comparative scope, bias from specialised synthetic or private datasets, and metrics that are "ambiguous" and "often tied to custom networks or downstream tasks". [V] — [TSGBench, arXiv 2309.03755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03755)
- A newer benchmark library (GenTS, 2026) exists. Contents not read. [V title] — [GenTS, arXiv 2605.17804](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17804v1)
- **Alpha-precision, beta-recall, authenticity** (Alaa, van Breugel, Saveliev, van der Schaar; ICML 2022). Alpha-precision is the probability that a synthetic sample lies in the alpha-support of the real distribution. Beta-recall is the fraction of real samples inside the beta-support of the generative distribution. Authenticity measures whether a sample is copied from training data, which is a memorisation test. The metrics also support post-hoc auditing (filtering bad samples). [V] — [How Faithful is your Synthetic Data?, PMLR 162, 2022](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/alaa22a/alaa22a.pdf)
- **TimeGAN**: Yoon, Jarrett, van der Schaar, "Time-series Generative Adversarial Networks", NeurIPS 2019. It introduced the discriminative and predictive scores. [M] — [NeurIPS 2019 paper page](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2019/hash/c9efe5f26cd17ba6216bbe2a7d26d490-Abstract.html)
- **PRDC**: Naeem, Oh, Uh, Choi, Yoo, "Reliable Fidelity and Diversity Metrics for Generative Models", ICML 2020. It introduced density and coverage to fix the outlier sensitivity of k-NN precision/recall. [M] — [arXiv 2002.09797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09797)
- **C2ST**: Lopez-Paz & Oquab, "Revisiting Classifier Two-Sample Tests", ICLR 2017. [M] — [arXiv 1610.06545](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.06545)
- **Context-FID origin**: Jeha et al., "PSA-GAN: Progressive Self Attention GANs for Synthetic Time Series", ICLR 2022. [M] — [arXiv 2108.00981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00981)

### Inferences
- The project already covers all four ML evaluation families: fidelity/diversity (PRDC), two-sample discrimination (C2ST), utility (TSTR) and privacy (memorisation). Known pitfalls that apply to this project:
  1. PRDC, C2ST and TSTR on short windows score **windows, not the 10-year process**, so they cannot detect long-memory or storm-chaining failures.
  2. Classifier and predictor scores vary with seed and architecture. Report several seeds and compare against the real-vs-real noise floor (which the project already does for statistics; extend it to C2ST/TSTR).
  3. The embedding choice for PRDC strongly affects the result. Raw zero-inflated rainfall makes k-NN distances dominated by dry windows, so condition on wet windows or use a transform.
  4. TSTR forecasting is a proxy, not the real downstream task (RL control).
- Combining domain statistics with ML sample-level metrics and a noise floor is uncommon in either literature. This is a defensible edge.

### Gaps
- I did not find a paper that applies PRDC, alpha-precision or C2ST to precipitation series. The absence is suggestive, not proven.
- TimeGAN, PRDC, C2ST and PSA-GAN citations are [M]. Verify the links.

## Q3. Synthetic rainfall in urban drainage design and CSO/RTC; Astlingen; RL for sewer control — training rainfall and the rainfall properties that matter

### Takeaway
In urban drainage, stochastic and continuous rainfall series are an established design tool: continuous simulation is preferred over design storms for CSO volume and frequency. RL-for-drainage papers, however, almost always train on **a few dozen to about 50 discrete events**: historical events sampled from the record, Chicago/IDF "designed" storms, or perturbed events. They do not train on long continuous stochastic series. On Astlingen, RL work trains on events sampled from the benchmark's own 2000-2009 5-min record. I found no RL paper that trains on a learned (deep generative) rainfall generator. Rainfall duration (long events), depth/volume and temporal pattern are the properties reported to change controller rankings.

### Cited Findings
**Synthetic rainfall in drainage/CSO design**
- Design-rainfall events should be limited to preliminary CSO threshold assessment. Final volume estimation for sizing should use continuous simulation. Synthetic storms built from several points of an IDF curve slightly underestimate CSO volumes (snippet). [V snippet] — [Selection of rainfall information as input data for the design of combined sewer overflow solutions, J. Hydrology 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169418306668)
- Continuous simulation gives the most accurate volume estimates and is sensitive to the number of simulated years. It is "probably the most reliable method for estimating the flow peak or volume for a given exceedance probability" (snippet). This is an argument for long synthetic series. [V snippet] — [Stochastic long term modelling of a drainage system with estimation of return period uncertainty, Water Sci. Technol. 59(12), 2009](https://iwaponline.com/wst/article/59/12/2331/13693/Stochastic-long-term-modelling-of-a-drainage)
- Climate-change CSO studies drive continuous rainfall-runoff simulations with synthetic precipitation series and find higher future overflow frequencies (snippet). [V title] — [Modeling climate change impacts on combined sewer overflow using synthetic precipitation time series, Water Sci. Technol. 68(1):160 (2013)](https://iwaponline.com/wst/article-abstract/68/1/160/17593/Modeling-climate-change-impacts-on-combined-sewer); [CLIMACS: stochastic generation of continuous climate-projected point rainfall for urban drainage design, J. Hydrology 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S002216942100826X) [V title]
- Onof & Arnbjerg-Nielsen (2009) and Arnbjerg-Nielsen (2012) combined two stochastic models to make 5-min point series from hourly RCM output for urban drainage (snippet from a secondary source; see the [STORAGE/related listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/241765245_A_simple_stochastic_rainfall_disaggregation_scheme_for_urban_drainage_modelling)). [M] Verify the original papers.

**Astlingen benchmark**
- Astlingen is a benchmark combined-sewer network designed by the DWA working group on integral RTC, complementing guideline DWA-M180. It is meant for setting up and evaluating RTC strategies and for teaching, and it ships with a simple example RTC algorithm. [V] — [Schütze, Lange, Pabst, Haas, "Astlingen – a benchmark for real time control (RTC)", Water Sci. Technol. (2018; PubMed 29851408)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29851408/) (author list [M])
- SWMM implementation with 6 storage tanks and 4 controllable orifices, made MPC-ready. [V] — [Sun, Lorenz Svensen, Borup, Puig, Cembrano, Vezzaro (2020), An MPC-Enabled SWMM Implementation of the Astlingen RTC Benchmarking Network, Water 12(4):1034](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/12/4/1034); [DTU/IRI PDF of the SWMM model](https://www.iri.upc.edu/files/scidoc/2332-A-SWMM-model-for-the-Astlingen-benchmark-network.pdf)
- RL on Astlingen: one study trained four RL agents and tested them under **10 real rainfalls** for flooding mitigation. Another (or the same group) used the open-source Astlingen SWMM's **5-min 2000-2009 rainfall**, randomly sampling **50 events from 2000-2006 for training** and one event (2006-08-22) for validation, and reported lower CSO volume than baseline. These snippets come from search results pointing at [Flooding mitigation through safe & trustworthy reinforcement learning, J. Hydrology 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169423003773) and [Multi-agent RL applications in the real-time control of an urban drainage system (2022/23)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366587915_Multi-agent_reinforcement_learning_applications_in_the_real-time_control_of_an_urban_drainage_system). Both pages returned 403, so **which detail belongs to which paper is uncertain**. Both appear to be from W. Tian / Z. Liao and co-authors [M]. Code: [MARL-UDS GitHub](https://github.com/soon2soon/MARL-UDS).

**RL for urban drainage: training rainfall**
- **Mullapudi, Lewis, Gruden, Kerkez (2020)**, "Deep reinforcement learning for the real time control of stormwater systems", Advances in Water Resources. DQN over 11 interconnected basins, trained across "diverse rainfall events", with 27 actions. [V] — [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10150026-deep-reinforcement-learning-real-time-control-stormwater-systems)
- **Saliba, Bowes, Adams, Beling, Goodall (2020)**, "Deep RL with uncertain data for real-time stormwater system control and flood mitigation", Water 12(11):3222. DDPG trained on flood-causing events **selected from 10 years of observations** (Norfolk, VA). Tested with perfect data, imperfect forecasts, and imperfect level plus forecast data. 70.5% average flood-volume reduction vs passive control. [V] — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/12/11/3222)
- **Bowes et al. (2021)**, "Flood mitigation in coastal urban catchments using real-time stormwater infrastructure control and reinforcement learning", J. Hydroinformatics 23(3):529. [V title] — [IWA](https://iwaponline.com/jh/article/23/3/529/77759/Flood-mitigation-in-coastal-urban-catchments-using)
- **Tian et al. (2022)**, WRR 58, e2021WR030703, "CSO and flooding mitigation through a reliable RTC based on multi-RL and MPC". A related Tian et al. (2022) WRR paper uses a Koopman operator with DRL ([e2021WR030939](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1029/2021WR030939)). Snippet: DQN and PPO agents trained on **50 designed rainfall events** for 500 episodes, outperforming in-place rule-based control, with DQN sensitive to changing rainfall. [V title; event count from snippet, not certain which Tian paper] — [WRR 2022](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2021WR030703)
- A pollution-based multi-agent DRL study (npj Clean Water 2025) states that **"due to the scarcity of long-term rainfall data, synthetic rainfall events are used"**. It uses "typical rainfall rotation" training over 5,000 episodes (snippet). [V snippet] — [npj Clean Water s41545-025-00512-z](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41545-025-00512-z)
- **Huang et al. (2025)**, "Dimensions of superiority: how DRL excels in UDS real-time control", Water Research X. Validated on 31 historical events (Suzhou). Robustness tested with 50 events carrying up to 20% forecast error. Adaptability tested with 100 generated events (50 varying pattern at fixed depth, 50 varying depth at fixed pattern). **Duration matters**: "When the rainfall duration is <5 h, MPC outperforms DRL and RBC in the flooding risk", but MPC "rapidly declined" for longer events, while DRL beat RBC regardless of duration. [V] — [PMC11850747](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11850747/)
- A Water Research 2024 review covers DRL challenges and opportunities for urban water systems, including data and generalisation issues (content not read). [V title] — [ScienceDirect S0043135424000459](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0043135424000459)
- **pystorms** (Rimer, Mullapudi, Troutman, et al.; Environmental Modelling & Software 2023; arXiv 2021) is a Python sandbox with "real world-inspired" stormwater control scenarios and quantitative scoring of control strategies. Whether Astlingen is a scenario, and what rainfall the scenarios use, could not be confirmed (PDF unreadable). From memory [M], scenarios ship with fixed single-event or short-period rainfall inputs, and a "gamma" scenario and others exist. [V metadata] — [arXiv 2110.12289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.12289); [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/2320223-pystorms-nbsp-simulation-sandbox-development-evaluation-stormwater-control-algorithms)
- Other DRL drainage studies: [multi-objective control of stormwater basins with DRL, J. Hydroinformatics 26(11):2852 (2024)](https://iwaponline.com/jh/article/26/11/2852/105668/Multiple-objective-control-of-stormwater-basins); [decentralised MARL for CSO and flooding, Water Research 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0043135422014439); [recurrent DRL framework for flood and CSO, Water Research 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0043135426004793) [V titles only].

### Inferences
- **Field norm:** RL drainage agents train on roughly 10-50 discrete events (historical, IDF/Chicago-designed, or perturbed), and the papers acknowledge rainfall scarcity. Training on **10 years of continuous, statistically validated synthetic 5-min rainfall** (inter-event dry periods, antecedent storage, storm chaining) appears to be new for RL in sewer control. This is likely the project's clearest edge.
- Rainfall properties that matter for control, from the evidence: event **duration** (long events flip MPC vs DRL rankings), **total depth/volume** and **temporal pattern** (the Huang 2025 adaptability tests), and **forecast uncertainty**. Antecedent conditions and back-to-back storms matter physically for tank-based systems like Astlingen (storage not yet emptied). I did not find an RL paper that tests this explicitly, which suggests it is under-studied. Continuous synthetic series exercise antecedent effects in a way that event-based training does not.
- Event-based RL work never validates its training rainfall statistically. The project's evaluation suite is far stricter than anything in the RL-for-drainage literature.

## Q4. Downstream / utility evaluations: generated rainfall judged by hydrological model outputs

### Takeaway
Utility evaluation through sewer models exists in the classical urban-drainage literature: synthetic vs observed rainfall compared on simulated CSO volume and frequency, flood volumes, and return-period flows from continuous simulation. It is not standard in generator papers; even a 2024 HESS paper lists it as future work. No deep generative rainfall paper I found uses hydrological outputs, and none evaluates rainfall by the performance of a controller trained on it.

### Cited Findings
- Stochastic model evaluation for extremes: when observed discharge records are short, extreme-value assessment relies on very long synthetic discharge series, and stochastic models are evaluated with **ensembles of many long synthetic series compared with observed ones** (snippet). [V snippet] — [Stochastic long term modelling of a drainage system, WST 2009](https://iwaponline.com/wst/article/59/12/2331/13693/Stochastic-long-term-modelling-of-a-drainage)
- Generators compared by feeding long event series into hydrodynamic sewer models and comparing CSO outputs from synthetic vs reference rainfall. System response assessed per event as **CSO discharged volume, peak overflow discharge and number of activated overflow structures** (snippet). [V snippet] — [J. Hydrology S0022169417304390 (2017)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169417304390); see also [Selection of rainfall information for CSO design, J. Hydrology 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169418306668)
- Hyetograph ensembles disaggregated from IDF curves are routed through a sewer network to simulate hydraulic performance (snippet). [V snippet] — [A simple stochastic rainfall disaggregation scheme for urban drainage modelling](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/241765245_A_simple_stochastic_rainfall_disaggregation_scheme_for_urban_drainage_modelling)
- STORAGE generator tested for urban flood analysis in Water Research (2024): "Possibility of using the STORAGE rainfall generator model in the flood analyses in urban areas" (abstract blocked; title only). [V title] — [Water Research S0043135424000356](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0043135424000356)
- A spatial-temporal point-process generator was evaluated with a fully distributed hydrological model in an urban drainage design context (snippet). [V title] — [A spatial-temporal rainfall generator for urban drainage design](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/245027284_A_spatial-temporal_rainfall_generator_for_urban_drainage_design)
- The 2024 HESS sub-hourly paper did **not** run a sewer or urban model and called for it. [V] — [Vorobevskii et al. 2024](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/28/391/2024/)
- Recent CSO work stresses continuous simulation under observed rainfall variability (e.g. SuDS retrofits giving up to 47% CSO volume reduction). This shows that the output metrics practitioners use are CSO volume, frequency and duration. [V snippet] — [Continuous simulation-based assessment of SuDS retrofit strategies for CSO mitigation, 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772411526000765)

### Inferences
- The project's "staged" plan, with an agent-in-the-loop test as a later milestone, sits between two existing practices. **Tier 1 (established):** run real and synthetic 10-year series through Astlingen SWMM under the baseline/static control and compare CSO volume, overflow frequency, flooding volume, tank-filling duration distributions and return-period overflows, using the real-vs-real noise floor as the yardstick. This mirrors the classical CSO-volume comparisons and is cheap. **Tier 2 (novel):** train RL on synthetic vs real data and test on held-out real years, which is TSTR at the control level. I found no precedent for Tier 2 in rainfall work.
- Adding Tier 1 would close the gap that reviewers in hydrology are most likely to raise ("statistics fine, but does it produce the right overflows?"), and it reuses the existing SWMM environment.

### Gaps
- Could not read the STORAGE Water Research paper or the 2017/2018 J. Hydrology CSO-comparison papers in full (403). Their exact metrics and results need confirming.
- Found no deep-learning rainfall generator evaluated through a hydrological or sewer model.
