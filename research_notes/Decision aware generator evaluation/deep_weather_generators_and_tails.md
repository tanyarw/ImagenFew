# Deep generative weather/rainfall generators used to test infrastructure or controllers, their failures on extremes, and tail-aware diffusion training (focus 2024-2026; deltas vs the 2026-10-01 review)

Notes compiled 2026-10-22, from about 35 web searches and fetches. They build on two earlier notes, `Rainfall generator literature review/deep_generative_rainfall.md` and `evaluation_and_downstream_use.md`, and do not repeat what those cover. Items from those notes appear here only when this session added new facts (for example, verified authors or numbers).

**Verification legend.** **[V]** means the content was read on a fetched primary page this session (arXiv abstract or HTML, or the conference PDF). **[S]** means it was seen only in search-result text (publisher pages for Wiley, ScienceDirect and others returned 403). **[M]** means it is from prior knowledge and was not re-fetched; check it before citing.

**Closeness ratings** are judged against the proposed headline: *"a scenario generator only needs to be accurate where the decision-makers (controllers or policies) disagree"*. The headline comes with a decision-weighted fidelity score that predicts whether generated scenarios rank policies as reality does, and a fix that selects or trains the generator for accuracy in that region.
- **SAME IDEA**: makes the same core claim, so the paper must state how it differs.
- **PARTIAL**: shares one half (the metric, the fix, or the premise).
- **DIFFERENT**: relevant context or method, but not the claim.

## Q1. Which deep weather/rain generators (2022-2026) are evaluated by a downstream impact model, and does any check that generated inputs give the same decisions or rankings as observed inputs?

### Takeaway
Still almost none. Beyond Ji et al. 2024 (GAN feeding SWAT, already in the 2026-10-01 notes), deep generators are validated on meteorological statistics, ensemble skill (CRPS, spread-skill) or, at most, cost-loss economic value (GenCast). **No deep generative weather or rainfall paper found tests whether its outputs rank designs or controllers the way observed weather does.** The closest decision-level checks come from three other places:
- **Classical stochastic-generator hydrology**: impact-based evaluation and tailored calibration from the Adelaide group (Bennett 2019, Nguyen 2023, McInerney 2025).
- **Energy scenario generation**: decision-focused training of VAE, GAN and diffusion scenario generators by dispatch cost (Zhou et al. 2026).
- **Robotics**: generative world models judged on policy-ranking agreement with real robots (WorldGym 2025 and similar).

### Cited Findings

**A. Deep generators with an impact or decision layer (2024-2026)**
- **[PARTIAL] Zhou, Zhou, Morstyn, Wang (2026), "Decision-Focused Scenario Generation and Selection for Efficient and Robust Grid Dispatch", arXiv 2607.05830.** The scenario generators can be VAEs, GANs or diffusion models. Instead of fitting the historical distribution, they are optimized by "their induced downstream operational cost" in distributionally robust dispatch. A differentiable selector then picks "decision-relevant scenarios" from the generated pool. The paper reports 0.80-2.02% lower operating cost than accuracy-oriented generators. It explicitly argues that accuracy-oriented training "can produce ambiguity sets that are statistically plausible but suboptimal for downstream operation." *Why PARTIAL:* this is the "fix" half of our headline (train or select the generator for decisions), but for one optimizer's cost, not for agreement in ranking several policies, and not tied to tails. **B. Classical stochastic generators evaluated through the impact model (the most relevant hydrology precedents, missing from the earlier notes)**
- **[PARTIAL] McInerney, Westra, Leonard, Kavetski, Thyer, Maier (2025), "Tailored calibration of stochastic weather generators for enhanced hydrological system evaluation", *Journal of Hydrology* 668** (MODSIM 2025 extended abstract read in full).
  - **Method.** The authors calibrate WGEN with the Simulated Method of Moments, *prioritising multi-day rainfall extremes* "as it is an important driver of hydrological response". Hydrological response is evaluated through GR4J in 3 NSW/ACT catchments.
  - **Results.** Conventional MoM/ML calibration "underestimates historical extreme multi-day rainfall by around 30%". This propagates to high-flow (99th percentile) streamflow biases of **30-50%**, which tailored calibration "substantially reduces". Robust Gauss-Newton optimisation cuts projected-streamflow uncertainty by up to 75%. The code uses the foreSIGHT R package and CAMELS-AUS.
  - *Why PARTIAL:* this is the "fix" half for a stochastic generator: calibrate it to be accurate on the attributes the system is sensitive to, namely extremes. The attributes are chosen a priori by hydrological judgement, though, and success is measured as streamflow bias, not policy ranking.
  - [V conference PDF; journal volume from S] — [MODSIM 2025 PDF](https://mssanz.org.au/modsim2025/files/K04.mcinerney.pdf); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022169425022358); [code DOI 10.25909/29346932.v1 via researchdata.edu.au](https://researchdata.edu.au/code-tailored-calibration-et-al/3888010)
- **[PARTIAL] Nguyen, Bennett, Leonard (2023), "Evaluating stochastic rainfall models for hydrological modelling", *Journal of Hydrology* 627A.** Two stochastic rainfall models (Markov-based and latent-variable) were evaluated at 277 Australian and 106 US stations. Both were rated "overall good" on rainfall metrics, but "streamflow-based metrics are not necessarily 'good'" when routed through GR4J. The authors argue that generator evaluation "is usually a stand-alone procedure" even though outputs feed infrastructure decisions. *Why PARTIAL:* this is direct evidence that statistic-level fidelity does not imply impact-level fidelity (our premise), but it stops at streamflow, not decisions. [S] — [Adelaide repository](https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/items/b94d5a1b-9633-4492-833c-e2752efa5883); [PDF](https://eres.library.adelaide.edu.au/dspace/bitstream/2440/140354/2/hdl_140354.pdf)
- **[PARTIAL] Bennett, Thyer, Leonard, Lambert, Bates (2019), "A virtual hydrological framework for evaluation of stochastic rainfall models", *HESS* 23, 4783.** The framework is streamflow-based, virtual (it avoids hydrological-model and data errors) and **targeted**: it isolates *which* sites and months of rainfall error matter for streamflow. It found that wetting-up-month rainfall matters across the whole year despite low flow volumes. *Why PARTIAL:* the method localises where generator error matters for the impact variable, which is the conceptual analogue of "where decisions disagree", but it does not involve decisions or policies. [S; author list beyond Bennett is M] — [HESS](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/23/4783/2019/)

### Inferences
- Deep generator papers report statistics, CRPS and spread-skill. Hydrology stochastic-generator papers report streamflow bias. Energy decision-focused papers report the cost of one optimizer. Robotics world-model papers report policy-ranking correlation. **Nobody combines a deep tail-weak rainfall generator, an exact simulator, several controllers, and a real-held-out ranking check.** That combination is the defensible gap.
- The Adelaide line (Bennett 2019 → Nguyen 2023 → McInerney 2025) is the strongest "must-cite and differ" in hydrology. They already argue that generator fidelity should be judged by impact-model response and that calibration should prioritise extremes. Our differences are three: (i) the weighting comes from *controller disagreement* rather than hydrological judgement; (ii) the target is *policy-ranking agreement* rather than streamflow bias; (iii) the generator is deep and uncontrolled (diffusion) rather than parametric.

## Q2. Known failures of deep generators on extremes and long dry/wet spells, and how papers measure them

### Takeaway
Failures fall into three groups:
1. **Gaussian-prior diffusion (EDM) under-generates heavy tails.** This is now quantified: kurtosis ratio 45 for EDM vs 0.5 for Student-t on HRRR VIL.
2. **Learned models cannot extrapolate beyond the training extremes.** This is the "gray swan" result: a FourCastNet trained without Category 3-5 storms cannot forecast Category 5 storms.
3. **Tail errors can go either way.** Downscalers underestimate moderate extremes and climate-change signals, but sometimes overestimate the most extreme events.

Spell persistence is rarely measured for deep generators. Where it is (daily, short windows), results are mixed, and nothing exists at sub-hourly resolution.

### Cited Findings
- **[DIFFERENT; method evidence] Pandey, Pathak, Xu, Mandt, Pritchard, Vahdat, Mardani, "Heavy-Tailed Diffusion Models", ICLR 2025 (arXiv 2410.14171).** Authors are now verified, resolving the uncertainty in the earlier notes.
  - **Data.** HRRR channels VIL (vertically integrated liquid, heavy right tail) and w20 (vertical velocity, heavy left tail). **No precipitation channel was used.**
  - **Metrics.** Kurtosis Ratio, Skewness Ratio, and a KS statistic on the tails.
  - **Results.** Unconditional, VIL: EDM gives KR 45.35 / SR 5.23 / tail-KS 0.991, vs t-EDM (ν=3) 0.54 / 0.23 / 0.114. w20: EDM KS 0.978 vs t-EDM 0.286. The paper states "standard EDM struggles to capture tails and extreme events" and attributes this to high-dimensional Gaussians concentrating on a thin shell.
  - **Caveats.** The best ν is channel-specific (ν=5 on VIL gives KR 5.73), and conditional t-EDM stays under-dispersed for VIL (SSR 0.255).
  - [V arXiv HTML; ICLR venue from NSF-PAR PDF header [S]] — [arXiv 2410.14171](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14171); [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10640550)
- **[DIFFERENT; failure evidence] Sun, Hassanzadeh, Weare, Abbot (2025), "Can AI weather models predict out-of-distribution gray swan tropical cyclones?", *PNAS*.** FourCastNet was retrained with Category 3-5 storms removed, globally or per basin, and tested on Category 5 storms from 2018-2023. Global skill is similar, but the model "could not accurately forecast Category 5 TCs", so it cannot extrapolate from weaker storms. There is partial cross-basin transfer, and physics constraints did not help. The authors argue the result likely extends to other AI weather and climate models. **The measurement protocol is useful for us: withhold the most extreme events from training and test on them.** [S] — [arXiv 2410.14932](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14932v3); [UChicago PDF](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/15273/files/sun-et-al-can-ai-weather-models-predict-out-of-distribution-gray-swan-tropical-cyclones.pdf)
- **[DIFFERENT] WassDiff, updated version (arXiv 2410.00381v4, retitled "Downscaling Extreme Precipitation with Wasserstein Regularized Diffusion").** The Wasserstein regulariser "greatly enhances" capture of extremes vs plain diffusion, yet the later version reports that the regularised model "slightly underestimates at extreme values". The fix narrows the tail gap without closing it. [S] — [arXiv 2410.00381v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00381v4)
- **[DIFFERENT] Direction of tail bias varies.** A snippet from 2025-2026 downscaling work notes a diffusion model that "slightly underestimates moderate extremes but overestimates most extreme events". The 2512.13987 intercomparison (already noted) finds most generative models underestimate the climate-change signal of extremes. So tail checks must be two-sided. [S] — [arXiv 2512.13987](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13987v2); [IPSL-AID, arXiv 2604.03275](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.03275)
- **[DIFFERENT] "Regional Climate Model Emulation with Diffusion Approaches: What is the Added Value of Generative ML?" (2026).** Diffusion emulators reproduce long-term precipitation statistics and show skill on extremes compared with a parametric model. [S] — [arXiv 2606.14570](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14570)
- **[DIFFERENT] Dry/wet-spell evidence for deep generators (daily, mixed):**
  - The Guevara et al. 2021 GAN/VAE comparison: "at dry observed days (0 mm/day) both VAE and GAN overestimate the wet days". This is a drizzle or occurrence bias that shortens dry spells. [S] — [arXiv 2107.08074](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08074)
  - "Loosely Conditioned Emulation of Global Climate Models With GANs" (NeurIPS 2020 CCAI workshop) reports accurate mean dry-day counts and mean longest dry spell, but only within **32-day** samples. [S; authors M: Puchko, Link, Hutchinson, Kravitz, Snyder] — [CCAI](https://www.climatechange.ai/papers/neurips2020/61)
  - Deep SPG v1.0 (Bird et al., *GMD* 16, 3785, 2023) is a single-site neural stochastic precipitation generator with an explicit wet-day probability output. It reproduces observed dry-spell lengths and seasonality. [S; title and authors M] — [GMD](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/16/3785/2023/)
- **[DIFFERENT] Classical generators share the extremes failure** (context for "this is not a deep-learning-only problem"). McInerney 2025 found conventional calibration underestimates multi-day extremes by about 30% (see Q1). [V] — [MODSIM PDF](https://mssanz.org.au/modsim2025/files/K04.mcinerney.pdf)

### Inferences
- Our failure (largest real storm 102 mm vs generator P95 of 43 mm) fits both the EDM thin-shell tail deficit (Pandey) and the gray-swan non-extrapolation result (Sun). With 10 years of data, the 102 mm storm is close to out-of-distribution: a generator trained on 10 years cannot be expected to reproduce it without an explicit tail mechanism.
- How tail failure is measured for diffusion: KR/SR/tail-KS (Pandey), upper-quantile Q-Q (GMD 2026), climate-signal of extremes (2512.13987), withheld-extreme tests (Sun). Spell persistence is measured mostly at daily resolution in short windows. Sub-hourly storm-chaining or inter-event-time failures appear unmeasured in the deep literature, so our storm-duration and inter-event diagnostics remain novel.

## Q3. Tail-aware and rare-event training and sampling for diffusion (2023-2026): does any improve a downstream decision metric?

### Takeaway
Methods fall into four families:
1. **Heavy-tailed noise**: Student-t (t-EDM), plus soft-log transforms (already noted).
2. **Loss and score reweighting**: class-balancing and minority-SNR weighting, mostly for image class imbalance, and multi-quantile losses with tail upweighting in deterministic downscalers.
3. **Sampling-time guidance toward rare regions**: minority guidance, low-density guidance, and guided cBottle with **importance-sampling odds-ratio correction**.
4. **Rare-event algorithms that use an AI emulator as the score**: AI+RES.

**None of these papers reports a downstream decision or impact metric.** The only evidence that a tail- or decision-targeted generator improves a downstream outcome comes from non-tail-specific work: Zhou et al. 2026 (dispatch cost, -0.8 to -2.0%) and McInerney 2025 (streamflow bias, classical generator).

### Cited Findings
- **[DIFFERENT; method] t-EDM / t-Flow (Pandey et al., ICLR 2025).** Student-t perturbation kernel and γ-divergence-inspired denoiser loss. Tail heaviness is set by one scalar (ν), and the authors say it "requires only minimal code changes" to EDM. Results are in Q2. [V] — [arXiv 2410.14171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14171)
- **[DIFFERENT; method, closest to a "tail fix + correct frequencies" recipe] Manshausen, Brenowitz, Berner, Kashinath, Pritchard (2026), "Towards accurate extreme event likelihoods from diffusion model climate emulators", arXiv 2605.03802 (v2, Oct 2026).** cBottle is *guided* toward tropical cyclones at target locations. The density ratio between guided and unguided models gives an **odds ratio used as an importance weight**, which reduces the standard error of event-probability estimates relative to plain Monte Carlo. The authors call the results "early but encouraging" and discuss the limits of using model densities for attribution-style experiments. A search snippet adds two caveats: at the highest threshold the IS estimate had 34% vs 41% relative uncertainty (small counts), and the odds ratio is expensive, so drawing more unguided samples can be cheaper. The snippet numbers are not verified on the abstract page. There is no decision use. [V abstract; numbers S] — [arXiv 2605.03802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03802); [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/extreme-event-likelihoods-with-guided-generative-models/)
- **[DIFFERENT; method] Um, Lee, Ye (2024), "Don't Play Favorites: Minority Guidance for Diffusion Models", ICLR 2024.** Classifier guidance on a "minority score" steers sampling toward low-density regions without labels. A follow-up, Park & Um, "Beyond Generative Priors: Minority Sampling with JEPA-Guided Diffusion", is listed at ICML 2026. A 2026 paper modifies reverse sampling for low-density exploration and frames "training-time reweighting" and "sampling-time corrections" as the two routes to rare samples. [S] — [arXiv 2301.12334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12334v2); [arXiv 2605.24631](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24631); [arXiv 2606.13347](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13347)
- **[DIFFERENT; method] Class-imbalance diffusion training.** CBDM (Class-Balancing Diffusion Models) notes that abrupt class re-sampling "easily leads to negative improvement" and uses a distribution-adjustment regulariser instead. TIDE adds class-adaptive minimum-SNR weighting. PoGDiff handles imbalanced text-to-image generation. These target *discrete* tail classes, not continuous magnitude tails. [S] — [CBDM arXiv 2305.00562](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.00562); [TIDE (UCF thesis)](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/etd2024/356/); [PoGDiff arXiv 2502.08106](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08106)
- **[DIFFERENT; method, transferable loss] "Multi-Quantile Regression for Extreme Precipitation Downscaling" (Q-SRDRN, arXiv 2605.12762, May 2026).** The paper argues that under intensity-weighted MAE, augmented extreme samples get "averaged into the predicted mean". It uses pinball loss up to τ=0.999 with extreme days upweighted 6x on the upper-tail heads (w=1+5·1[y>threshold]). It is deterministic, not diffusion. [S] — [arXiv 2605.12762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12762)
- **[DIFFERENT; method] AI+RES: "AI-boosted rare event sampling to characterize extreme weather" (arXiv 2510.27066; EGU25 abstract by Lancelin, Wikner, Hassanzadeh, Abbot, Bouchet, Dubus, Weare).** AI-emulator ensemble forecasts act as the score function for splitting/killing rare-event sampling of a physics model (PlaSim). It gives accurate rare-event statistics with up to about O(100)x speed-up and reaches once-per-millennium heatwaves, where standard rare-event sampling "fails to even yield examples". [S] — [arXiv 2510.27066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27066v1); [EGU25-9087](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU25/EGU25-9087.html)
- **[DIFFERENT] Diffusion extreme-scenario generation with an asymmetric loss for long-tailed weather (MS-CGDM, *Energy* 2025).** See Q1. [S] — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/energy/v336y2025ics0360544225041349.html)
- **Survey context.** "A Survey of Synthetic Data Generation for Rare Events" (2025) lists importance sampling biased toward extremes as one of six categories. Its examples are mostly GAN-based (for example GMM-GAN latents biased toward extremes, with importance weights). [S] — [arXiv 2506.06380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.06380)
- **[PARTIAL] Rare-event-focused testing of controllers with an unbiasedness correction: Feng et al. (2023), "Dense reinforcement learning for safety validation of autonomous vehicles", *Nature* 615, 620-627.** Background agents trained on naturalistic data produce adversarial, safety-critical manoeuvres. The MDP is "densified" by removing non-critical states, giving test speed-ups of about 10^3-10^5 while the authors claim **unbiased** controller evaluation. *Why PARTIAL:* the scenario generator is concentrated where it matters for evaluating a controller (rare critical events) and then corrected to stay unbiased. That is the infrastructure-testing analogue of "accurate where it matters", but the goal is failure-rate estimation for one controller, not ranking agreement. [S; the IS formulation is from memory, M] — [DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-05732-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05732-2); [UMich project page](https://traffic.engin.umich.edu/research/automated-vehicle-system-testing-and-evaluation)

### Inferences
- For 5-min rainfall with EDM, the most transferable options, in order of code cost:
  1. A Student-t prior (t-EDM): a single ν, "minimal code changes", and shown on HRRR VIL, a precipitation-like heavy right tail.
  2. Loss upweighting of high-intensity windows (Q-SRDRN style), keeping the clipping and EMA caveats from MEMORY in mind.
  3. Guidance toward large storm depth **plus importance-weight correction** (Manshausen), so that expected controller performance stays unbiased.

  Option 3 is the one that matches "accurate where controllers disagree": oversample the disagreement region, then reweight.
- If storms are oversampled for decision evaluation without likelihood correction, policy rankings will be biased toward big-storm performance. Feng 2023 and Manshausen 2026 both stress that the correction is required. The paper's fix should report rankings under the corrected (reweighted) expectation.
- **Novel empirical claim available:** no tail-aware diffusion paper shows that tail improvement changes a downstream decision or ranking. Showing that t-EDM or tail reweighting restores controller-ranking agreement on held-out real years would be new.

### Gaps
- No precipitation-trained t-EDM result was found (HRRR VIL is a proxy).
- The full Manshausen 2026 numbers and limits are not read beyond the abstract.
- No paper was found that combines an EVT/GPD tail *inside* a diffusion sampler for precipitation. A search for "generalized Pareto diffusion" returned nothing directly. The EVT-GAN family (already noted) remains the only EVT-deep hybrid.
- Not verified: whether Feng 2023 uses explicit likelihood-ratio importance sampling or a related unbiasedness construction (memory says NADE plus importance sampling).

## Q4. Work linking generator or simulator accuracy to decision quality or policy ranking (decision-relevant extremes, impact-based evaluation)

### Takeaway
The *general* idea that "a model only needs to be accurate where it matters for decisions" is well established in four places:
- **Model-based RL**: value-aware model learning, value equivalence, objective mismatch, VaGraM. This is the closest SAME IDEA in theory.
- **Decision-focused learning**: SPO.
- **Water-resources decision scaling**: Brown 2012, where climate information is needed only for states where decisions or thresholds flip.
- **Robotics**: sim-to-real predictivity (Kadian 2020's SRCC, tuning a simulator to maximise policy-ranking correlation), and generative world models validated by policy-ranking agreement (WorldGym 2025, Veo, Runway).

McPhail et al. 2020 gives important counter-evidence: in the Lake Problem, scenario choice changed robustness *values* strongly but rankings only slightly. **No work was found that (a) weights generator fidelity by *pairwise controller disagreement*, (b) shows that such a score predicts ranking agreement across many generator variants, and (c) ties it to tail failure of a deep generator.** That combination is the paper's defensible novelty.

### Cited Findings

**Theory: model accuracy where it matters for value or decisions**
- **[SAME IDEA (conceptual)] Value-Aware Model Learning (Farahmand, Barreto, Nikovski, AISTATS 2017).** The model is trained so that its Bellman backup matches the true model's, ignoring dynamics that do not affect value. A 2023 survey describes it as letting the model "learn any dynamics that match the true environment in terms of value estimation, without spending capacity on irrelevant details". *Why SAME:* this is the theoretical form of "accurate only where it matters for decisions". We differ in three ways: the input generator is exogenous rather than a dynamics model; the task is ranking a *fixed set* of policies rather than optimising one; and the weighting is by policy disagreement rather than value. [S via survey; paper link M] — [survey arXiv 2310.06253](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.06253); [PMLR v54 (M)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v54/farahmand17a.html)
- **[SAME IDEA (conceptual)] Voelcker, Liao, Garg, Farahmand (ICLR 2022), "Value Gradient weighted Model-Based RL" (VaGraM).** It weights model error by the value gradient, which is a *localised* decision-weighted fidelity. It helps most with small model capacity, which is exactly our finite-capacity situation. The paper notes that earlier value-aware methods "tended to underperform maximum likelihood baselines in practice". *Why SAME:* fidelity weighted by decision relevance, with capacity as the motivation. The difference is the weighting signal (value gradient for one agent vs controller disagreement). [S] — [arXiv 2204.01464](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.01464v2)
- **[PARTIAL] Related theory** [titles from search; content M]:
  - Grimm, Barreto, Singh, Silver (NeurIPS 2020), "The Value Equivalence Principle for Model-based RL": models need to be equivalent only on a set of policies and functions. This is very close to "accurate only for the policies we compare". — [arXiv 2011.03506 (M)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03506)
  - Lambert, Amos, Yadan, Calandra (2020), "Objective Mismatch in Model-based RL". — [arXiv 2002.04523 (M)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04523)
  - Survey: "A Unified View on Solving Objective Mismatch in MBRL" (2023). — [arXiv 2310.06253](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.06253)

  The value-equivalence *set of policies* is arguably the nearest formal statement of our headline. It deserves a direct comparison paragraph.
- **[PARTIAL] Elmachtoub & Grigas (2022), "Smart 'Predict, then Optimize'", *Management Science*.** The decision-focused loss (SPO+) for predictors is the ancestor of decision-focused scenario generation (Zhou 2026, Diff2SP). *Why PARTIAL:* point prediction for one optimiser, not generators or rankings. [M] — [arXiv 1710.08005 (M)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08005)

**Simulators and world models judged by policy-ranking agreement**
- **[PARTIAL; closest on the metric] Kadian et al. (2020), "Sim2Real Predictivity: Does Evaluation in Simulation Predict Real-World Performance?", IEEE RA-L (arXiv 1912.06321).**
  - **Metric.** The paper defines **SRCC**, the Pearson correlation between paired sim and real performance of several policies.
  - **Results.** For Habitat (CVPR19 settings) SRCC for success was 0.18, because agents exploited collision dynamics. **Tuning simulator parameters raised it to 0.844.**
  - **Critique.** A review notes that SRCC requires real rollouts and that Pearson captures only linear agreement.
  - *Why PARTIAL:* the target quantity (does the simulator rank policies as reality does, and can we tune it to?) is exactly our ranking-agreement objective, with real held-out ground truth. It is not localised to a disagreement region, though, and it tunes a physics simulator, not a stochastic input generator.
  - [S] — [arXiv 1912.06321](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06321v2); [Reality-gap review arXiv 2510.20808](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.20808)
- **[PARTIAL] Quevedo, Sharma, Sun, Suryavanshi, Liang, Yang (2025), "WorldGym: World Model as An Environment for Policy Evaluation", arXiv 2506.00613.** An action-conditioned video world model with VLM rewards. Policy success rates "highly correlate with real-world success rates", and the model "preserves relative policy rankings across policy versions, sizes, and training checkpoints". *Why PARTIAL:* the generative model is validated by policy-ranking agreement, but there is no tail or disagreement weighting. [V abstract] — [arXiv 2506.00613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00613)
- **[PARTIAL] Other world-model ranking results.**
  - Runway GWM-Robotics blog: Pearson 0.95 between sim and real success across 8 VLA policies, plus a *Mean Maximum Rank Violation* metric.
  - "Evaluating Gemini Robotics Policies in a Veo World Simulator" (Google, 2025): Pearson 0.86, with predicted absolute success rates consistently lower than real ones.
  - WorldEval: 0.79, secondary source only.

  These are blog or secondary sources with few policies each. [S] — [Runway](https://runwayml.com/research/accelerating-robot-policy-evaluation); [Veo summary](https://liner.com/review/evaluating-gemini-robotics-policies-in-veo-world-simulator)
- **[PARTIAL] Yu, Zhang, Sheng, Ren, Lin (2026), "How Should World Models Be Evaluated for Embodied Decision-Making? A Decision-Making-Centric Position", arXiv 2606.15032.** The paper argues "the more decisive issue is not whether the model generates visually convincing videos". It proposes an L0-L7 evidential ladder that includes **"policy-ranking agreement, optimization lift, model exploitability"**, and flags a "claim/evidence mismatch" in the literature. *Why PARTIAL:* the same evaluation philosophy (decision usefulness over realism), stated generally and without a localised fidelity score. [V abstract] — [arXiv 2606.15032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15032)

**Water resources: decision-centric scenarios and ranking sensitivity**
- **[PARTIAL; conceptual precursor] Brown, Ghile, Laverty, Li (2012), "Decision scaling: Linking bottom-up vulnerability analysis with climate projections in the water sector", *WRR* 48, W09537.** Three steps: model the decision and identify thresholds; use stochastic (weather-generator) analysis to find the climate states associated with risk; *then* tailor climate information to estimate the probabilities of those states. Brown's slides argue that GCMs "are inefficient samplers ... for vulnerability analysis". *Why PARTIAL:* climate information is needed only where decisions or thresholds are sensitive, which is the classic water-sector version of the headline. It does not, however, score generator fidelity or test policy-ranking agreement. [S] — [PDF (SIWI copy)](https://siwi.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/brown-et-al-2012_decision-scaling.pdf)
- **[DIFFERENT] Steinschneider & Brown (2013), WRR.** A multisite daily weather generator "for use in decision-centric vulnerability assessments" (wavelet AR, Markov chain with k-NN, quantile mapping). It is a stress-test tool, not a fidelity-to-decision link. [S] — [PDF](https://websites.umass.edu/hydrosystems/files/2017/05/Steinschneider-and-Brown-2013.pdf)
- **[PARTIAL; important counter-evidence] McPhail, Maier, Westra, Kwakkel, van der Linden (2020), "Impact of Scenario Selection on Robustness", *WRR* 56(9), e2019WR026515.** On the Lake Problem, scenario selection "has a large influence on the robustness value" of each alternative but "a relatively small influence on how those decision alternatives are ranked". The effect arises from interactions with the system model and the robustness metric. *Why PARTIAL:* this is the ranking-agreement test applied to scenario-set choice. Its finding cautions that rankings can be insensitive to input-scenario errors, so our paper must show that Astlingen rankings *do* flip under tail-deficient generators. [S] — [deepuncertainty.org](https://www.deepuncertainty.org/2020/09/11/impact-of-scenario-selection-on-robustness-2020); [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_3099760e-f997-4cdc-9d59-121c4c047659)
- **[PARTIAL; premise support] A forecast-informed reservoir operations study for California (ASCE *JWRM*), "Variability, Attributes, and Drivers of Optimal Forecast-Informed Reservoir Operating Policies...".** It reports that large floods are needed in the training data to avoid overfitting the policy. This supports the premise that policy quality hinges on extremes in the training inputs. [S] — [ASCE](https://ascelibrary.org/doi/abs/10.1061/JWRMD5.WRENG-6471)
- **[PARTIAL; premise support, already in the 2026-10-01 notes] Huang et al. (2025), *Water Research X*.** MPC beats DRL and RBC for storms under 5 h, but declines for longer ones. Controller rankings flip with storm attributes. — [PMC11850747](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11850747/)

**Positioning**
- **What the paper cannot claim:** that accuracy should be judged by decisions (VAML, SRCC, decision scaling, world-model position papers all do), or that generators should be calibrated toward extremes (McInerney).
- **What it can claim (no precedent found):**
  1. A fidelity score weighted by *pairwise controller disagreement*, computed per storm or window in an exact simulator.
  2. A meta-evaluation across ~28 generator variants showing this score predicts real-vs-generated ranking agreement (Kendall τ, or SRCC-style plus rank-violation) better than unweighted statistics, C2ST or PRDC.
  3. The empirical link that controllers disagree mostly in big storms, which is exactly where diffusion generators fail (EDM thin-shell tails, gray-swan non-extrapolation).
  4. A tail fix (t-EDM, reweighting, or guidance plus importance correction) evaluated by restored ranking agreement. No tail-aware diffusion paper reports a downstream decision metric.
- **Reviewer risks:**
  - (a) "This is value equivalence." Answer: an exogenous generator, policy-set disagreement weighting, an empirical predictive test, and no access to gradients.
  - (b) McPhail-type robustness: rankings may not flip. Show flips, or report when they don't.
  - (c) SRCC on few policies is fragile. Use rank metrics, bootstrap over held-out years, and report Runway-style maximum rank violation.
  - (d) Oversampling extremes biases expected-performance rankings unless importance-corrected (Feng, Manshausen).
