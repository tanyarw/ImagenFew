# Real-plus-synthetic inference for evaluating and ranking policies (prediction-powered inference and related methods)

Scope: methods that combine a few real samples with many model-generated or synthetic samples to estimate, compare or rank models and policies with valid uncertainty, and whether they apply when the synthetic samples are **unpaired draws from a generator** (the thesis setting: about 2 held-out real rainfall years, unlimited generated years that are biased, for example 40% less overflow, and an exact SWMM simulator that scores every controller on any rain series).

Closeness ratings used throughout:
- **SAME IDEA**: makes the thesis headline claim, or proposes the thesis method, in essentially the same form.
- **PARTIAL**: shares a central component (the estimator, the data design, or the fidelity-on-decisions idea) but differs in a way a paper must state.
- **DIFFERENT**: a tool or adjacent literature to cite, not a competitor.

Verification note: all venues, years and arXiv IDs below were checked in this session through search results, arXiv abstract pages or Crossref, except where a line says "not verified". Some summaries of methods come from abstracts or search-engine summaries rather than full text, and those lines say so.

## Q1. Prediction-powered inference (PPI) and its extensions: what do they assume?

### Takeaway
Every member of the PPI family (PPI, PPI++, cross-PPI, stratified PPI, active inference, recalibrated PPI, post-hoc regression PPI) assumes **paired data**: a small labelled set in which each real unit carries both its true outcome and the model's prediction for that same unit, plus a large unlabelled set of **real inputs** drawn from the same distribution, on which only predictions are computed. The variance gain comes entirely from the per-unit correlation between the true outcome and the prediction. None of these methods accepts unpaired synthetic inputs from a generator as the "unlabelled" pool.

### Cited Findings
- **[DIFFERENT, foundational tool] Angelopoulos, Bates, Fannjiang, Jordan, Zrnic, "Prediction-powered inference", *Science* 382(6671):669–674, 2023.** It provides provably valid confidence intervals for means, quantiles, and linear and logistic regression coefficients by combining a small labelled ("gold-standard") dataset with machine-learning predictions on a large dataset, without assumptions on the ML model. More accurate predictions give narrower intervals. Closeness: it is the estimator the thesis method would build on, but it assumes real unlabelled inputs and paired predictions, not generated scenarios. — [arXiv 2301.09633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09633); Science volume, issue and pages checked via [Crossref record for DOI 10.1126/science.adi6000](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1126/science.adi6000); online 9 Nov 2023 per [UC Berkeley news](https://vcresearch.berkeley.edu/news/how-use-ai-discovery-without-leading-science-astray)
- **[DIFFERENT, tool] Angelopoulos, Duchi, Zrnic, "PPI++: Efficient prediction-powered inference" (arXiv 2023).** It adds a power-tuning parameter λ to the objective: classical loss plus λ times (prediction-based loss on the unlabelled data minus prediction-based loss on the labelled data). The estimator is unbiased for any λ. λ=1 recovers PPI, λ=0 recovers classical inference, and a data-tuned λ "always improve[s] on classical intervals" asymptotically. It is implemented as `lam=None` in the `ppi_py` package. Closeness: this is the tuning that would let a biased generator (for example a uniform 40% overflow deficit) still help, because λ rescales the prediction, but it still needs pairing. — [arXiv 2311.01453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01453); [ppi_py API docs](https://ppi-py.readthedocs.io/en/latest/ppi.html)
- **[DIFFERENT, tool] Zrnic & Candès, "Cross-prediction-powered inference", *PNAS* 121(15):e2322083121, 2024.** When no pre-trained predictor exists, it trains the predictor on the labelled data with cross-fitting and then debiases. It is "consistently more powerful" than splitting off part of the labelled data for training. Closeness: relevant only if the thesis wanted to use the same real years both to train the generator and to correct it, which would require K-fold retraining of the diffusion model. — [arXiv 2309.16598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16598); [PNAS DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2322083121)
- **[DIFFERENT, tool] Zrnic & Candès, "Active statistical inference", ICML 2024 (PMLR 235:62993–63010).** A model chooses which points to label under a budget, and the intervals and tests stay valid for any black-box model. Closeness: it assumes a pool of real unlabelled units to choose from. In the thesis the scarce resource is the real rain itself, not labels, so it does not transfer. — [arXiv 2403.03208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03208); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/zrnic24a.html)
- **[DIFFERENT, tool] Fisch, Maynez, Hofer, Dhingra, Globerson, Cohen, "Stratified prediction-powered inference for hybrid language model evaluation", NeurIPS 2024.** Stratifying the data improves basic PPI considerably when the autorater's quality varies across strata, and valid intervals are derived for parameters of any dimension. Closeness: a tool for stratifying by season or storm type if the generator's fidelity varies by regime. — [arXiv 2406.04291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04291); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/c9fcd02e6445c7dfbad6986abee53d0d-Abstract.html)
- **[DIFFERENT, tool] Ji, Lei, Zrnic, "Predictions as surrogates: revisiting surrogate outcomes in the age of AI" (arXiv 2025).** It connects PPI to the classical surrogate-outcome model and proposes **recalibrated PPI**, which learns the optimal "imputed loss" with flexible ML and always improves on the labelled-only estimator, even when that loss is estimated imperfectly. Summary from abstract and search snippet. — [arXiv 2501.09731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09731)
- **[DIFFERENT, but critical for n≈2] Eyre & Madras, "Regression for the mean: auto-evaluation and inference with few labels through post-hoc regression", ICML 2025 (PMLR 267).** The paper first appeared as "Auto-evaluation with few labels through post-hoc regression". PPI++ can perform **worse than classical inference when labelled data are scarce**, because its λ-tuning amounts to an OLS regression with high variance at small n. The authors propose robust-regression variants for the few-label regime. Closeness: this warns directly about the thesis regime, where 2 real years means tiny n. — [arXiv 2411.12665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12665); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/eyre25a.html)
- **[DIFFERENT, theory link] Gronsbell, Gao, McCaw, Shi, Cheng, "Another look at statistical inference with machine learning-imputed data" (arXiv 2024, v7 2026).** It recasts "prediction-based inference" with the two-phase sampling literature and a Z-estimation approach aimed at matching or beating classical efficiency, and it claims connections to foundational statistical methods. The abstract does not name AIPW explicitly. — [arXiv 2411.19908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19908)
- **SureSim (see Q3)** states the general condition explicitly: combining data helps "only when the rectifier variance is smaller than the variance of real evaluations." — [arXiv 2510.04354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04354)

### Inferences
- **Why pairing is essential (derived from the standard PPI++ mean estimator, not quoted from a paper).** Take the mean estimator θ̂ = Ȳ_n + λ(f̄_N − f̄_n). With optimal λ, Var = Var(Y)/n · (1 − ρ²·N/(N+n)), where ρ = corr(Y, f(X)) is computed on the *same* unit. As N → ∞ the effective real sample size is multiplied by 1/(1−ρ²): about 5.3× at ρ=0.9, about 2× at ρ=0.7, and about 1.3× at ρ=0.5. If the synthetic draws in the correction term are independent of the real units (unpaired), the cross-covariance term disappears, Var = Var(Y)/n + λ²Var(f)(1/N + 1/m), and the optimal λ is 0. **Unpaired synthetic samples cannot reduce the variance of an unbiased estimator of a real-distribution mean.** They can only trade bias for variance (see Q3, Oberst et al.) or rely on outside calibration (see Q3, task exchangeability).
- **The thesis setting reverses PPI's usual cost structure.** In PPI, inputs X are cheap and real, and labels Y are expensive. In the thesis, the label is free: SWMM is exact, so any rain series gives every controller's overflow. The input, real rain, is what is scarce. So the classical "unlabelled pool of real X" does not exist unless some **cheap real covariate Z** is available in long records, for example daily or hourly totals from long gauge or reanalysis records. A generator conditioned on Z then produces a "synthetic twin" of each Z (see Q4).
- **The unit of analysis matters more than the estimator.** With about 2 real years as units, no CLT-based or betting-based interval will be informative. In practice, units must be storm events or months within the held-out years, with block structure for antecedent sewer storage. Even then, the Eyre & Madras result implies that a fixed λ, or a robust or shrunk λ, is preferable to a freely tuned PPI++ λ.
- **Independence requirement.** PPI needs the predictor (here the generator) to be independent of the labelled real data. The generator was trained on 10 years that exclude the 2 held-out years, so standard PPI applies to the held-out years. Reusing training years as labelled data would need cross-PPI-style refitting.

### Gaps
- I did not open the full PPI paper to check whether its covariate-shift or label-shift extensions would allow the unlabelled inputs to come from a different (generated) distribution with known density ratios. A diffusion generator would not give such ratios in any case.
- No paper found quantifies PPI with n below about 10 labelled units. Eyre & Madras study the few-label regime, but I did not check their smallest n.

## Q2. Prediction-powered ranking and LLM-as-judge evaluation with real-label correction: how are rank sets with coverage built?

### Takeaway
Chatzi et al. (NeurIPS 2024) is the reference method for **rank-sets with coverage** from a few gold comparisons and many proxy comparisons. It builds a PPI confidence ellipsoid for the vector of model win-probabilities and turns it into simultaneous rank intervals with asymptotic coverage. Like the rest of the LLM-judge line (AutoEval done right, stratified PPI, mixed-subjects designs), it needs the proxy and the gold label on the **same instances**. A separate strand (JuStRank; Dorner, Nastl & Hardt) shows that a judge's value for *ranking systems* differs from its instance-level accuracy, and that debiasing gains are fundamentally limited.

### Cited Findings
- **[PARTIAL] Chatzi, Straitouri, Thejaswi, Gomez-Rodriguez, "Prediction-powered ranking of large language models", NeurIPS 2024.**
  - *Data.* There are two sets: D_N with LLM comparisons only, and D_n (n ≪ N) where the **same instance** (a query plus a pair of responses) has both a human and LLM comparisons. Queries and comparisons are assumed i.i.d. — [arXiv 2402.17826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17826)
  - *Estimand and region.* θ_m is the probability that model m's output is preferred over a uniformly random other model's output. The PPI estimate θ̂ = a − b combines an LLM-only average with a human-minus-LLM correction weighted by λ, which is chosen to minimise the trace of the covariance. The confidence region is an ellipsoid with a χ² quantile. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17826v3)
  - *Rank-sets.* For each pair of models, the method checks whether the ellipsoid lies entirely on one side of the hyperplane θ_i = θ_j. Pairs that are decided bound each model's rank interval [l̂(m), û(m)]. Theorem 4.1 gives **simultaneous, asymptotic** coverage of all true rank-sets with probability ≥ 1−α. Finite-sample (PAC) guarantees are left as future work. It builds on simultaneous rank confidence intervals (Al Mohamad et al.; Neuhof & Benjamini) rather than Mogstad et al. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17826v3)
  - *Bias.* LLM–human mismatch shows up as wider rank-sets, not as invalid coverage. LLM-only rank-sets were often inconsistent with human ones on Chatbot Arena data. — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/cd47cd67caa87f5b1944e00f6781598f-Abstract-Conference.html)
  - Closeness: this is the closest *ranking-with-coverage* method, but its proxy is a judge applied to real paired instances, not a generator of new unpaired scenarios.
- **[PARTIAL] Boyeau, Angelopoulos, Yosef, Malik, Jordan, "AutoEval done right: using synthetic data for model evaluation", ICML 2025 (PMLR 267).** It uses PPI++ to estimate evaluation metrics from AI-generated labels plus a few human labels. Applications include ranking computer-vision models, evaluating protein-fitness predictors, and Chatbot Arena pairwise comparisons. It reports up to a 50% increase in effective human-labelled sample size with GPT-4. Here "synthetic data" means **synthetic labels on real inputs**, not synthetic inputs. Closeness: it is a model-ranking application of PPI, but it is paired. — [arXiv 2403.07008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07008); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/boyeau25a.html)
- **[PARTIAL] Broska, Howes, van Loon, "The mixed subjects design: treating large language models as (potentially) informative observations", *Sociological Methods & Research* 54(3):1074–1109, 2025.** It extends PPI to experiments that mix humans and LLM "silicon subjects". It defines a **"PPI correlation"** as a measure of how interchangeable predictions and human data are, and gives a power analysis for choosing between human and LLM samples. An R package, `mixedsubjects`, implements it. Closeness: it names a fidelity number that governs PPI gains, which is a direct precedent for a "fidelity score that predicts usefulness", but the score is defined on raw outcomes rather than decision contrasts. — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/somere/v54y2025i3p1074-1109.html); [package docs](https://cran.r-universe.dev/mixedsubjects/doc/introduction.html)
- **[PARTIAL to headline] Dorner, Nastl, Hardt, "Limits to scalable evaluation at the frontier: LLM as judge won't beat twice the data", ICLR 2025.** When the judge is no more accurate than the model being evaluated, **no debiasing method can cut the required ground-truth labels by more than half**. Measured savings on MMLU and MT-Bench were smaller than this bound. Closeness: a hard ceiling on real-plus-proxy gains that the thesis must discuss. The thesis analogue is that gains are capped by the generator's fidelity on exactly the compared quantities. — [arXiv 2410.13341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13341)
- **[PARTIAL to headline] Gera et al. (IBM Research), "JuStRank: benchmarking LLM judges for system ranking", ACL 2025.** Judges are scored by how well their **system-level ranking** matches the human Chatbot Arena ranking, not by instance-level accuracy (48 judges). It finds system-specific judge bias and a "decisiveness" trait: some judges consistently amplify the differences between systems. Closeness: it argues that evaluator quality should be measured at the decision (ranking) level, which supports the headline, but it is purely empirical and has no inference. — [arXiv 2412.09569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09569); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.34)
- **[DIFFERENT, rank-inference machinery] Mogstad, Romano, Shaikh, Wilhelm, "Inference for ranks with applications to mobility across neighbourhoods and academic achievement across countries", *Review of Economic Studies* 91(1):476–518, 2024.** Confidence sets for ranks of populations ranked on estimated features. It is a classical (real-data-only) alternative to the Chatzi construction. — [DOI 10.1093/restud/rdad006](https://doi.org/10.1093/restud/rdad006)

### Inferences
- The Chatzi recipe carries over directly once paired twins exist (Q4). Let θ be the vector of controller values (mean overflow) or the K−1 contrasts against a baseline controller. Build the PPI ellipsoid from real held-out events plus their twins and many generated twins, then read off rank-sets. Because every controller is run on the **same** rain, the covariance matrix is dominated by the shared scenario component, and the contrasts will have far smaller variance than the levels (common random numbers, see Q5).
- Chatzi's guarantee is asymptotic in n. With about 2 years, or a few dozen events, a finite-sample construction would be needed. SureSim's betting intervals (Q3) are the available finite-sample template for bounded outcomes, combined with a union bound over pairs.
- The "decisiveness" finding in JuStRank maps onto a multiplicative generator bias, for example 40% less overflow. Such a bias can preserve the ranking while shrinking every gap. PPI++'s λ rescales a proxy whose contrasts are proportionally shrunk, so a uniformly scaled bias costs little variance if per-event contrasts stay correlated.

### Gaps
- I did not find any paper that applies prediction-powered ranking to RL policies or controllers rather than LLMs or vision models.
- The finite-sample rank-set extension that Chatzi et al. list as future work: I did not find a published follow-up in this session.

## Q3. Policy evaluation and simulators (control variates, sim-to-real correction, doubly robust OPE) and the new "synthetic-data inference" methods (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
There are three families.
- **(a) Paired sim-plus-real PPI for policy evaluation.** SureSim (2025) is the closest method precedent: per-policy confidence intervals from paired real and simulator trials, with 20–25% hardware savings. SCAPE (2026) learns a scenario-conditioned correction. Classical doubly robust OPE and multifidelity Monte Carlo have the same "model plus residual correction" structure and also need pairing.
- **(b) Unpaired synthetic-data inference (2025–2026).** GESPI uses a guardrail and lets error rise to α+ε. Task-exchangeability methods (Tan & Zrnic; Huang, Wu, Wang; Huang & Wang) calibrate the real–synthetic gap on *historical tasks* that have real data. GMM-based estimators (Byun et al.) are a third route. These are the only frameworks that accept unpaired generator draws, and each buys validity with an extra assumption or an error slack.
- **(c) Biased-plus-unbiased estimator combination** (Oberst et al.; Lin, Bickel, Ding) and **ranking and selection under input uncertainty** from simulation optimisation. These are the closest operations-research and statistics framings of "few real inputs plus a fitted input model".

### Cited Findings
**(a) Paired simulator-plus-real policy evaluation**
- **[PARTIAL, closest method precedent] Badithela, Snyder, Zha, Mikhail, O'Kelly, Dixit, Majumdar, "Reliable and scalable robot policy evaluation with imperfect simulators" (SureSim), arXiv Oct 2025.**
  - Each real environment is mapped to a simulated counterpart by a real2sim function, and "a small number of paired real and simulation evaluations are used to rectify bias in large-scale simulation".
  - Estimators are uniform PPI and a two-stage PPI. Intervals use non-asymptotic Waudby-Smith–Ramdas betting. A hedged version (SureSim-UB) takes a union bound with the classical interval.
  - Each real label is the average over 5 real initial conditions and is paired with the average over 100 simulated initial conditions in a small neighbourhood.
  - It saves **over 20–25% of hardware evaluation**, but there is **no gain at low real–sim correlation** (ρ≈−0.05 for π0). The authors note that "reliably predicting the real evaluation outcome for a specific initial condition is extremely challenging".
  - It estimates per-policy means. There is no dedicated ranking procedure.
  - Closeness: it is the thesis method minus ranking, with a hand-built simulator in place of a learned scenario generator.
  - — [arXiv 2510.04354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04354)
- **[PARTIAL] Zhu, Oh, Huang, Huang, Ma, Tang, "SCAPE: scenario-conditioned simulation-augmented policy evaluation", arXiv Aug 2026.** It trains a correction model on paired (scenario, real metric, sim metric) data, uses it to pseudo-label simulation-only scenarios ("correction-before-augmentation"), and fits a scenario-conditioned predictor of real performance. Uncertainty comes from split conformal prediction, which gives marginal coverage only. It selects planners scenario by scenario (nuPlan driving, Unitree Go2) rather than producing a global ranking. It also uses PPI as a baseline. Closeness: it uses the same "scenario-conditioned twin" idea, but its target is per-scenario prediction, not policy ranking. — [alphaXiv / arXiv 2608.19425](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.19425)
- **[PARTIAL, weak] Xu et al., "Beyond binary success: a diagnostic meta-evaluation framework for fine-grained manipulation" (MetaFine), arXiv May 2026.** Its abstract describes "hybrid real-sim validation" in which "limited paired real-world rollouts" calibrate simulation estimates. It names no statistical method and explicitly moves "from ranking to diagnosis". — [arXiv 2605.19986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19986)
- **[PARTIAL] Konyushkova et al. (DeepMind), "Active offline policy selection", NeurIPS 2021.** Biased off-policy-evaluation (OPE) estimates serve as the prior, and a Gaussian process over policies (with a kernel encoding policy similarity) chooses which few policies to evaluate online. It beats both OPE alone and purely online evaluation, including on real robotics. Closeness: it combines few real evaluations with many biased estimates to select among policies, but the uncertainty is Bayesian, there is no frequentist rank-set coverage, and evaluation is per policy rather than per scenario. — [arXiv 2106.10251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10251)
- **[DIFFERENT] Thomas & Brunskill, "Data-efficient off-policy policy evaluation for reinforcement learning" (MAGIC), ICML 2016.** It extends the doubly robust (DR) estimator by blending model-based and importance-sampling estimates. — [arXiv 1604.00923](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00923). Closeness: DR/OPE solves the *counterfactual-action* problem, logged data under a different policy. In the thesis, rain is exogenous and SWMM can replay any controller on any rain, so the off-policy issue is absent. Only the scarcity of exogenous scenarios remains.
- **[DIFFERENT/PARTIAL] Chae, Nwankwo, Qin, Zhou, "Optimal sequential annotations for off-policy evaluation", arXiv Sep 2026.** Doubly robust OPE with missing rewards: cheap imperfect labels (LLM judges, classifiers) on every record plus an optimised budget of expert annotations. The abstract reports RMSE reductions and does not explicitly claim interval coverage. — [arXiv 2609.26707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26707)
- **[DIFFERENT, numerical-analysis cousin] Peherstorfer, Willcox, Gunzburger, "Survey of multifidelity methods in uncertainty propagation, inference, and optimization", *SIAM Review* 60(3):550–591, 2018.** Cheap low-fidelity models accelerate estimation while the high-fidelity model "stays in the loop". Multifidelity Monte Carlo is a control-variate estimator that evaluates both models at the **same random inputs**, which is pairing by construction. — [DOI 10.1137/16M1082469](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1082469); [preprint](https://cims.nyu.edu/~pehersto/preprints/multi-fidelity-survey-peherstorfer-willcox-gunzburger.pdf)
- **[PARTIAL, twin construction] Digital twins and PPI in clinical trials.**
  - PPI has been adapted to randomised trials using disease-progression-model prognostic scores. The estimator is described as asymptotically unbiased for the average treatment effect, it is compared with ANCOVA on an Alzheimer's trial, and the authors call it "akin to digital twin methods". — [medRxiv 2025.01.15.25320578](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.01.15.25320578); [PMC11759613](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11759613/)
  - PROCOVA uses a generative model trained on historical controls to produce a per-patient "digital twin" prognostic score as a covariate. Variants: Weighted PROCOVA [arXiv 2309.14256](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14256) and Bayesian PROCOVA [arXiv 2310.18027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18027).
  - Closeness: this is the clearest existing precedent for "a generator produces a twin of each real unit, conditioned on that unit's observed covariates, and inference stays unbiased whatever the generator's quality".
- **[PARTIAL to headline] Robot-policy world-model evaluators** (for example RoboWorld, arXiv Jul 2026) report agreement between neural-simulator and real leaderboards. RoboWorld's abstract (via search summary, not opened) reports Pearson 0.989 and Spearman 0.970 with the real leaderboard. These come with no statistical guarantee. — [arXiv 2607.01060](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01060)

**(b) Unpaired synthetic-data inference (accepts generator draws)**
- **[PARTIAL, most relevant unpaired framework] Bashari, Lee, Lotan, Dobriban, Romano, "General synthetic-powered inference" (GESPI), arXiv 2025 (v3 Jun 2026), listed as an ICML 2026 poster.**
  - It wraps any base procedure and runs it three times: real-only at level α, a real-only "guardrail" at α+ε, and pooled real-plus-synthetic at α. The outputs are combined with set operations.
  - Synthetic data are i.i.d. from an **arbitrary** distribution Q, need not be paired, and need no distributional assumptions.
  - Finite-sample risk ≤ α + min{ε, c·d}, where d is a distance between the real and synthetic distributions. With poor synthetic data the user "pays" at most ε.
  - Tasks covered: conformal prediction and risk control, one-sided tests (including **LLM win-rate comparisons**), and FWER multiple testing. Mean estimation and ranking are not main applications.
  - Stated limitation: "the power gain depends on the quality of the synthetic data."
  - Closeness: the only general framework that accepts unpaired generator draws in a real-data task. It does not rank policies and needs the ε slack.
  - — [arXiv 2509.20345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20345); [ICML 2026 listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/61182); [OpenReview (earlier title "Statistical inference leveraging synthetic data with distribution-free guarantees")](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Ww3cow5dO3)
- **[DIFFERENT/PARTIAL] Bashari et al., "Synthetic-powered predictive inference" (arXiv 2025).** A predecessor of GESPI for conformal prediction with synthetic data. Details not verified here. — [arXiv 2505.13432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13432)
- **[PARTIAL] Tan & Zrnic, "Valid inference with synthetic data via task exchangeability", arXiv 2026 (v2 Aug 2026).**
  - It assumes **no real data for the task of interest**. Real data exist for *historical tasks* that are exchangeable with the current one.
  - Method: build a CI for the synthetic-data estimand, compute "real–synthetic gap" intervals on the historical tasks, and shift the synthetic CI by quantiles of those gaps.
  - Coverage ≥ 1−α, with a finite-sample version upper-bounded by 1−α+2/(T+1). Under non-exchangeability, coverage degrades by weighted total-variation penalties.
  - Synthetic draws are i.i.d. per task, and per-individual pairing is not required.
  - Closeness: it is unpaired and valid, but it needs a population of comparable historical tasks.
  - — [arXiv 2606.13629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13629)
- **[PARTIAL] Huang, Wu, Wang, "Uncertainty quantification for LLM-based survey simulations", ICML 2025.** It turns imperfect LLM-simulated responses into confidence sets for human population parameters by **choosing the simulation sample size adaptively** (too many synthetic samples give over-narrow, under-covering sets). Coverage is average-case, and the chosen sample size doubles as a measure of LLM–population misalignment. An early draft was titled "How many human survey respondents is a large language model worth?". — [arXiv 2502.17773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17773); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/huang25am.html)
- **[PARTIAL] Huang & Wang, "Learning a size–weight frontier for synthetic-augmented inference", arXiv Aug 2026.**
  - It chooses the synthetic sample size k and a per-sample weight λ jointly, learning from historical tasks a frontier of (λ, k) that keeps **task-marginal** coverage (averaged over tasks, weaker than task-conditional coverage).
  - Synthetic samples are independent simulator draws, conditionally independent of the real data given the task, and the simulator may be misspecified.
  - There is uniform coverage over the frontier, with error O(J^−1/2) in the number of historical tasks J.
  - On WorldValuesBench it reports 34–53% narrower intervals at nominal coverage.
  - — [arXiv 2608.28576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28576)
- **[PARTIAL/DIFFERENT] Byun, Gupta, Lipton, Childers, Wilder, "Valid inference with imperfect synthetic data", NeurIPS 2025** (arXiv title "Using imperfect synthetic data in downstream inference tasks"). It is a hyperparameter-free generalized-method-of-moments estimator that treats proxy and synthetic data as auxiliary sources, each with its own parameter. The gains come from "interactions between the moment residuals of synthetic data and those of real data". Summary from abstract. Whether its synthetic samples must be generated per real unit (a form of pairing) was **not verified**. — [arXiv 2508.06635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06635); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ede6376c80b38796c688a77dce9fb227-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **[DIFFERENT] Lu, Wang, Zhang, Zhang, "Generative augmented inference of LLM-generated data for market research: theory and empirical evidence" (GAI), arXiv Apr 2026.** It treats AI outputs as high-dimensional *features* for predicting human labels rather than as label surrogates. It is more efficient than human-only estimation under random labelling, which is still a paired design. — [arXiv 2604.14575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14575)
- **[DIFFERENT] Decruyenaere, Dehaene, et al., "Debiasing synthetic data generated by deep generative models", NeurIPS 2024.** Deep-generative-model synthetic data induce bias and inflate type-I error. Targeted or debiased ML corrections make synthetic-data inference **asymptotically equivalent to real-data inference** when the synthetic sample is much larger than n. The goal is privacy-preserving release, not gaining information beyond the real data. — [arXiv 2411.04216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04216)
- **[PARTIAL] van Breugel, Seedat, Imrie, van der Schaar, "Can you rely on your model evaluation? Improving model evaluation with synthetic test data" (3S Testing), NeurIPS 2023.** It generates synthetic test sets (for small subgroups or simulated shifts) to evaluate ML models, with intervals from deep generative ensembles. Its stated assumption is that the "generative model fits the data", and there is no real-label correction. Closeness: it uses a generator as the evaluation set, which is the thesis practice the headline critiques, without validity under generator bias. — [arXiv 2310.16524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16524)
- **[DIFFERENT, background review] Abdel-Azim, Wang, Lin, "Harnessing synthetic data from generative AI for statistical inference", arXiv Mar 2026 (submitted to *Statistical Science*).** A review of pitfalls (misspecification bias, understated uncertainty) and "emerging frameworks for the principled use of synthetic data". I read only the abstract. — [arXiv 2603.05396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05396)

**(c) Biased-plus-unbiased combination, and input uncertainty in simulation**
- **[PARTIAL, unpaired route] Oberst, D'Amour, Chen, Wang, Sontag, Yadlowsky, "Understanding the risks and rewards of combining unbiased and possibly biased estimators, with applications to causal inference", arXiv 2022 (rev. 2023).** It combines a high-variance unbiased estimator (here, real years) with a low-variance, possibly biased one (here, generated years). Key concepts are the **bias threshold** below which combining helps, and an MSE-minimising weighting whose MSE is within a small multiple of the unbiased estimator's MSE for any bias. — [arXiv 2205.10467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10467)
- **[PARTIAL] Lin, Bickel, Ding, "Introducing the b-value: combining unbiased and biased estimators from a sensitivity analysis perspective", arXiv 2026.** Inference with unknown bias via a sensitivity parameter (the "b-value"). It recommends soft-thresholding as the most robust of three canonical combinations. Summary from search snippet. — [arXiv 2602.16310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16310)
- **[PARTIAL, closest OR framing] Ranking and selection under input uncertainty.**
  - Simulation input models fitted to finite real data propagate error into the outputs, so "the best system under the estimated model may not be the real-world optimum". — [Kim & Song, arXiv 2502.16659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16659)
  - The "most probable best" (Bayesian posterior over input models). — [Kim, Kim, Song, arXiv 2207.07533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07533)
  - Distributionally robust selection of the best picks the alternative with the best worst-case mean over a set of plausible input distributions, with probability-of-correct-selection guarantees. — [Fan, Hong, Zhang, *Management Science* 66(1):190–208, 2020; arXiv 1903.05828](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05828)
  - Closeness: this is the same problem structure (pick the best controller when the scenario distribution is learned from finite data), but it uses parametric or Bayesian input models and never corrects a learned generator with held-out real samples.

### Inferences
- **SureSim and the digital-twin trial methods are the two precedents a reviewer will bring up first.** The thesis must say what it adds: (i) a learned *scenario generator* (exogenous inputs) instead of a plant simulator, (ii) *ranking or contrasts* across controllers rather than per-policy means, and (iii) a fidelity score defined on decision contrasts.
- **The structural fact is that unpaired generator draws cannot give an unbiased, lower-variance estimate on their own** (Q1 derivation). The available validity-preserving routes for unpaired draws are:
  1. accept an error slack ε (GESPI);
  2. import a bias calibration from exchangeable historical tasks (Tan & Zrnic; Huang et al.). For the thesis, "tasks" might be other catchments or gauges where both real and generated records exist. Different controller pairs on the same years are not independent tasks, so they are speculative as tasks;
  3. bound the bias a priori (Oberst; b-value);
  4. create pairing with a conditional generator (Q4).

  Route 4 is the only one that gives both unbiasedness and variance reduction without an extra assumption on the generator.
- **The OPE literature's main difficulty (counterfactual actions) is absent in the thesis.** The thesis problem is closer to *input uncertainty* in stochastic simulation plus *multifidelity control variates* than to off-policy evaluation. Citing DR/OPE is still needed because DR, AIPW and PPI share the same algebra.
- **A likely reviewer question.** If the generator was trained on 10 real years, why not evaluate the controllers on those 10 years? Decruyenaere et al. show that synthetic data from a generator trained on n points can at best match inference on those n points. The thesis answer must be that the training years are contaminated for evaluation (for example, controllers or hyperparameters were tuned on them) or that generated years add useful variation (extremes). This should be made explicit.

- No paper found applies PPI, GESPI or task-exchangeability to hydrology, urban drainage, climate or weather-generator outputs. One targeted search returned only emulator and simulation-based-inference papers without real-data correction. This looks like an open niche, but the search was not exhaustive.
- The SCAPE details and Byun et al.'s pairing requirement were read from abstracts and summaries only.
- I did not verify the specific WSC papers on input uncertainty (Song, Nelson & Hong 2015; Wu & Zhou 2017). Search results attribute to them the finding that input uncertainty can "either force users to revise their objectives or have little effect on selecting the best", but I did not open them, so they are not cited as primary sources here.

## Q4. Is there work that uses unpaired generated scenarios (or a conditional generator making a synthetic twin of each real scenario) plus a few real scenarios to rank policies with valid uncertainty?

### Takeaway
**No paper found does exactly this.** The combination of (i) a learned generator of exogenous scenarios, (ii) a few real scenarios, (iii) many policies or controllers, and (iv) rank-sets or contrast intervals with coverage did not appear in any search. The pieces exist separately: paired twin construction (SureSim's real2sim map, SCAPE's scenario conditioning, PROCOVA digital twins), ranking with coverage (Chatzi et al.), and unpaired validity (GESPI, Tan & Zrnic). The thesis method contribution would be the assembly: a **conditional generator that produces a synthetic twin of each real event, PPI on controller contrasts, and Chatzi-style rank-sets**. Its novelty claim should be scoped to "learned exogenous-scenario generators" and "ranking", because paired sim-plus-real PPI for policies already exists (SureSim).

### Cited Findings
- **Paired twin via a mapping from real to simulated instances, per-policy CIs, no ranking:** SureSim uses a real2sim function g, runs real and simulated trials on the same instance, and averages 100 simulated initial conditions per real label. — [arXiv 2510.04354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04354)
- **Scenario-conditioned correction, conformal, per-scenario routing:** SCAPE. — [arXiv 2608.19425](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.19425)
- **Per-unit generative "digital twin" conditioned on baseline covariates, unbiased by design:** PROCOVA and PPI for clinical trials. — [medRxiv 2025.01.15.25320578](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.01.15.25320578); [arXiv 2309.14256](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14256)
- **Ranking with coverage, paired proxy:** Chatzi et al. — [arXiv 2402.17826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17826)
- **Unpaired draws allowed, guardrail, includes pairwise win-rate tests:** GESPI. — [arXiv 2509.20345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20345)
- **Unpaired draws, no target-task real data, calibration from historical tasks:** Tan & Zrnic — [arXiv 2606.13629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13629); Huang, Wu, Wang — [arXiv 2502.17773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17773); Huang & Wang — [arXiv 2608.28576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28576)
- **Few real evaluations plus many biased estimates to *select* a policy (Bayesian):** Active offline policy selection. — [arXiv 2106.10251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10251)

### Inferences
- **A concrete paired design for the thesis (proposal; not found in the literature for rainfall).**
  1. Pick a cheap real covariate Z with long real records, for example daily or hourly rain totals from long gauge or reanalysis records, if such records exist for the Astlingen setting. The repository's two-resolution generator (`tworesolution/`, coarse-to-fine) is a natural conditional generator X | Z.
  2. For each real held-out event i: Y_i = vector of controller overflows on the real 5-min rain, and f_i = mean controller overflow over M generated twins conditioned on Z_i. Averaging M twins mirrors SureSim's 100 simulated initial conditions per real label.
  3. For the N real-Z-only periods: f_j = mean over twins.
  4. The PPI++ estimate of the controller-contrast vector is valid regardless of generator bias, provided the held-out events are independent of generator training. Its variance gain is set by ρ_Δ = corr(real contrast, twin contrast) per event.
  5. Chatzi-style rank-sets follow from the ellipsoid.
  6. Caveat: the unlabelled Z must come from the same distribution as the labelled Z. If Z itself is generated, the design falls back to the unpaired case.
- **This turns the headline into a theorem-shaped statement.** In the paired design, the precision gain for ranking depends only on how well twins reproduce the **per-scenario contrasts between controllers** ("accurate where decision-makers disagree"), not on the marginal overflow level. A uniform 40% level bias is absorbed by λ. The thesis's "decision-weighted fidelity score" could therefore be defined as, or validated against, ρ_Δ or the rectifier variance of the contrasts. This links the fidelity score to an operational quantity, effective real sample size ≈ n/(1−ρ_Δ²). The link is my inference from the standard PPI++ variance formula. No paper found states it for scenario generators.
- **Without a cheap real Z, the honest options are:** (a) GESPI-style guardrailed tests of "controller A beats B" at α+ε; (b) a sensitivity analysis over a bias bound (Oberst; b-value); (c) task-exchangeability calibration if comparable historical tasks exist (for example other catchments or gauge records with both real and generated years).

## Q5. Ranking with biased evaluators: when does a common bias leave rankings unchanged, and how can that be tested?

### Takeaway
A bias that is **common to all policies** (additive, or any strictly increasing transform of each policy's true value) leaves the ranking unchanged. Only **policy-by-scenario interaction**, meaning bias in the *contrasts*, can flip ranks. Existing literatures measure this at the decision level:
- robotics sim-to-real (SRCC, MMRV);
- OPE benchmarks (rank correlation, regret@k);
- LLM-judge system ranking (JuStRank);
- clinical surrogates (trial-level surrogacy, the surrogate paradox);
- online experiments (proxy metrics judged by directional agreement of effects).

None provides a formal test of "rank preservation" for a scenario generator. The natural test is on the contrast rectifier, and in an equivalence form rather than a null-hypothesis form.

### Cited Findings
- **[PARTIAL to headline] Kadian et al., "Sim2Real predictivity: does evaluation in simulation predict real-world performance?", IEEE RA-L 2020.** It defines the Sim-vs-Real Correlation Coefficient (SRCC), the Pearson correlation of methods' sim and real performance across n methods. SRCC for success was 0.18 for Habitat as used in the CVPR19 challenge and was improved to 0.844 by tuning simulator parameters. Closeness: simulator fidelity is defined by whether it ranks methods as reality does, which is the headline's evaluation criterion, without a statistical test. — [arXiv 1912.06321](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06321)
- **[PARTIAL to headline] Li et al., "Evaluating real-world robot manipulation policies in simulation" (SIMPLER), CoRL 2024.** It uses over 1,500 paired sim and real evaluations. **Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV)** counts sim-vs-real ordering violations between policy pairs and **weights each violation by the real performance margin** (per search summary). Google Robot visual matching scored Pearson 0.924 and MMRV 0.056, against 0.308 and 0.375 for a validation-MSE baseline. Closeness: MMRV is an existing decision- and margin-weighted fidelity metric that the thesis score must be compared with. — [arXiv 2405.05941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05941); [PMLR v270](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/li25c.html)
- **[PARTIAL to headline] Fu et al., "Benchmarks for deep off-policy evaluation", ICLR 2021.** OPE estimators are evaluated for both value estimation and **policy selection**. The exact definitions of rank correlation and regret@k were not verified from full text in this session. — [arXiv 2103.16596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16596)
- **[PARTIAL to headline] Buyse, Molenberghs, Burzykowski, Renard, Geys, "The validation of surrogate endpoints in meta-analyses of randomized experiments", *Biostatistics* 1(1):49–67, 2000.** It separates *individual-level* agreement between surrogate and true endpoint from *trial-level* agreement between **treatment effects** on the two, which allows prediction of the true effect from the surrogate effect. Closeness: this is the direct biostatistics analogue of "fidelity on the decision-relevant contrast, not on the outcome". — [Hasselt repository](https://documentserver.uhasselt.be/handle/1942/366)
- **[PARTIAL to headline] VanderWeele, "Surrogate measures and consistent surrogates", *Biometrics* 69(3):561–565, 2013.** The **surrogate paradox**: treatment improves the surrogate and the surrogate correlates with the outcome, yet treatment harms the outcome. It requires an opposing direct effect, surrogate–outcome confounding, or lack of transitivity. Proportion explained, Prentice criteria and meta-analytic approaches do not fully protect against it. Closeness: it formalises why outcome-level fidelity (a generator matching rainfall statistics) does not guarantee correct decision ranking. — [DOI 10.1111/biom.12071](https://doi.org/10.1111/biom.12071)
- **[PARTIAL to headline] Tripuraneni, Richardson, et al., "Choosing a proxy metric from past experiments" (arXiv 2023; the search summary says KDD 2023, venue year not verified).** Proxy quality combines **sensitivity** and **directional alignment** of treatment effects with the long-term metric across a historical corpus of A/B tests. The optimal proxy depends on the experiment's sample size (noise level). — [arXiv 2309.07893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07893)
- **[PARTIAL] JuStRank:** system-level judge bias and "decisiveness" (amplified differences) as separate failure modes from instance accuracy. — [arXiv 2412.09569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09569)
- **[DIFFERENT, variance basis] Glasserman & Yao, "Some guidelines and guarantees for common random numbers", *Management Science* 38(6):884–908, 1992.** It gives conditions (monotonicity, continuity) under which common random numbers (the same random inputs across compared systems) reduce variance, or are optimal, when comparing stochastic systems by simulation. — [DOI 10.1287/mnsc.38.6.884](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.38.6.884)
- **[PARTIAL, headline, outside this scope] Grimm, Barreto, Singh, Silver, "The value equivalence principle for model-based reinforcement learning", NeurIPS 2020.** Two models are value-equivalent with respect to a set of policies and functions if they give the same Bellman updates. The more policies and functions are included, the more the equivalent class shrinks towards the true model. Closeness: a theoretical form of "the model only needs to be right for what the decision-makers' values depend on". — [arXiv 2011.03506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03506)
- **Input-uncertainty ranking and selection:** input-model error "may" make the estimated best differ from the real-world optimum. — [arXiv 2502.16659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16659)

### Inferences
- **The condition for rank invariance.** Write the generator-based value as V_syn(π) = V_real(π) + b(π).
  - The ranking is preserved iff, for every pair (j,k), sign(Δ_jk + b_j − b_k) = sign(Δ_jk).
  - Sufficient: b(π) is constant across π (additive common bias), or V_syn = g(V_real) with g strictly increasing (for example a uniform multiplicative shrink, such as 40% less overflow if the shrink is proportional across controllers).
  - Necessary and sufficient pairwise: |b_j − b_k| < |Δ_jk| whenever the two have opposite signs.
  - So what matters is the **bias of the contrasts**, compared against the **size of the real gaps**. With seed-to-seed gaps of 0.02–0.04 of baseline overflow, the contrast bias must be below that scale. A 40% level bias is irrelevant if it is common, and decisive if it varies by controller.
- **How to test it with paired twins.**
  - The PPI rectifier for each contrast, r_i = Δ_real,i − Δ_twin,i, is an unbiased per-event estimate of contrast bias.
  - Test the joint null that all mean contrast biases are zero (Hotelling or permutation test over pairs).
  - Better, run a **TOST-style equivalence test** that |mean contrast bias| < δ_jk, with δ_jk set to half the smallest decision-relevant gap. "Ranks are preserved" is an equivalence claim, and failing to reject zero bias at n≈2 years is not evidence.
- **How to test it without twins.** Real contrasts (computed on real events, all controllers on the same rain, so common random numbers apply within the source) and synthetic contrasts (computed on generated events) form two independent samples, and a Welch-type two-sample test on mean contrasts applies. Power is limited by the real n, which is why twins matter for the headline evaluation as well as for the estimator.
- **The headline's novelty relative to SRCC, MMRV and trial-level surrogacy.** Those metrics are computed *after* observing real rankings across many methods or trials. A thesis contribution would be a score computable from the generator and the controllers, possibly with few real events, that *predicts* real rank agreement. Example: an estimated ρ_Δ, or the rectifier variance, or a margin-weighted MMRV computed against the real held-out year. The thesis could show this predictive relationship across the ~28 generator variants.

### Gaps
- No paper found gives a formal hypothesis test of "ranking preserved by a biased simulator or generator" with coverage. The equivalence-test construction above is my proposal.
- The exact MMRV definition (normalisation, max over which pairs) was taken from a search summary, not the SIMPLER full text. It should be checked before the thesis defines a competing metric.
- I did not find work on rank stability under *multiplicative* (scale) bias of a scenario generator specifically. The JuStRank "decisiveness" finding is the nearest empirical analogue.
