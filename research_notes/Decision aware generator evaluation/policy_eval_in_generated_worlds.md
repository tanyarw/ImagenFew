# Policy evaluation inside learned or generated worlds (robotics world models, sim-to-real ranking, AD scenario generation)

Scope note (2026-10-22): judged against the thesis headline "a scenario generator only needs to be accurate where the decision-makers (controllers/policies) disagree". Closeness ratings (SAME IDEA / PARTIAL / DIFFERENT) are given inline in square brackets and collected in a table under Key Question 2. No work found rates SAME IDEA. Links marked "(prior knowledge)" are well-known papers whose arXiv IDs I did not re-fetch in this session. Check them before citing.

## Q1. Robotics: how do SIMPLER and the 2025-2026 world-model policy evaluators validate agreement, what do they find, and do they explain which errors matter?

### Takeaway
Robotics validates simulated and generated-world evaluators almost entirely by **per-policy success-rate agreement on small policy sets** (3-8 policies). The two metrics are **Pearson r** and **Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV, from SIMPLER)**, with Spearman sometimes added. Reported agreement is high (r of about 0.78-0.99), and absolute success is often biased (usually under-estimated) while rankings survive. Every paper explains failures **qualitatively**: contact physics, hallucinated objects, missing retries, under-represented failure modes, autoregressive drift, and "everyone fails" regimes. **None offers a theory of which world-model errors matter for ranking.** The only formal ideas are SIMPLER's MMRV, which weights violations by the real gap, and a 2026 position paper.

### Cited Findings
- **SIMPLER** (Li et al., arXiv 2405.05941, May 2024) defines RankViolation(i,j) = |R_i − R_j| · 1[(R_S,i < R_S,j) ≠ (R_i < R_j)] and MMRV = (1/N) Σ_i max_j RankViolation(i,j). Each mis-ranking is weighted by its **real-world margin**, so near-ties cost little. The authors prefer it to Pearson because Pearson "only assesses the linear fit" and "does not reflect the range of values". **[PARTIAL]**: MMRV is decision-weighted on the *outcome* side (it penalises flips in proportion to how much the real policies differ), but it does not weight *simulator error* by where policies differ. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941v1)
- SIMPLER, Google Robot results: Visual Matching gives MMRV 0.056 and Pearson 0.924. Variant Aggregation, which uses larger visual shifts, gives MMRV 0.143 and Pearson 0.778. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941v1)
- SIMPLER control gap: PD gains were fitted by system identification. SysID gave control loss 0.131 and MMRV 0.031, against MMRV 0.070 and 0.100 for perturbed controllers. Visual gap: green-screen plus texture matching together gave MMRV 0.050. Applying only some components gave **no improvement**. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941v1)
- SIMPLER, a biased simulator that still ranks correctly: mismatches in physical properties (mass, friction) shifted success rates by up to about 15% but kept MMRV low. The authors say the goal is "not to obtain a 1:1 reproduction" but correct relative ordering, and that a linear relationship is not required. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941v1)
- **WorldGym** (Quevedo, Sharma, Sun, Suryavanshi, Liang, Yang; arXiv 2506.00613; ICLR 2026) is an autoregressive action-conditioned video model with a VLM reward. Per-task success correlates with real success at r = 0.78, and mean success is within 3.3% of real. It preserves the ranking of RT-1-X, Octo and OpenVLA, of larger and newer versions, and of training checkpoints. The authors acknowledge that "generating highly realistic object interaction remains challenging". The project page is titled "Evaluating Robot Policies in a World Model". **[DIFFERENT]**: correlation reporting with no error theory. — [project page](https://world-model-eval.github.io/abstract); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00613v2)
- **WorldEval** (Li, Zhu, Wen, Shen, Xu; arXiv 2505.19017, May 2025) uses Policy2Vec to make a video model follow latent actions. It claims to rank policies and checkpoints, to flag dangerous actions, and to show "strong correlation" with the real world, without numbers in the abstract. The reported figures **conflict**: a third-party summary gives average Pearson 0.942 and MMRV 0.044, while another summary gives Pearson 0.79. Verify these against the paper's tables. **[DIFFERENT]** — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19017); [Pith summary](https://pith.science/paper/2505.19017)
- **Ctrl-World** (Guo, Shi, Chen, Finn; arXiv 2510.10125; ICLR 2026) is a multi-view DROID world model tested on 3 policies (π0, π0-FAST, π0.5) and 7 tasks. Its ranking of instruction following matches reality on most tasks; on Stack, real success ties at 0.80 but the model ranks π0-FAST (0.70) above π0.5 (0.65). It **systematically under-estimates success**: for π0.5, Close-Laptop is 0.70 real vs 0.05 imagined and Drawer is 0.80 vs 0.30. The gaps are attributed to imprecise contact physics, missing real-world retries, and failure modes under-represented in DROID. PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID and FVD are reported but **not linked to ranking accuracy**. **[DIFFERENT]** — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10125v3)
- **Veo world simulator** (Gemini Robotics Team, arXiv 2512.10675, Dec 2025/Jan 2026) was validated with 1600+ real trials, 8 policy checkpoints and 5 bimanual tasks. Nominal ranking is accurate, but predicted absolute success is **lower** than real. On the generalisation axes, Policy A gives Pearson 0.86 and MMRV 0.06. On the **object axis all policies had low success, making them hard to distinguish**. Hallucinated objects appear during gripper contact. The paper has **no analysis of which errors drive ranking** and no fidelity weighting. **[DIFFERENT]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10675v2)
- **Scalable Policy Evaluation with Video World Models** (Tseng, Gu, Zhang, Mao, Liu, Shkurti, Lin; NVIDIA; arXiv 2511.11520) reports Pearson/MMRV for Cosmos-Predict2-2B: Lift 0.879/0.015, Can 0.860/0.141, Square 0.847/0.165, Tool Hang 0.833/0.217. **Removing policy rollouts from world-model training** dropped Square's Pearson from 0.847 to 0.505, with FVD rising from 98.5 to 130.2. Pearson and MMRV can diverge: without rollouts, Lift's Pearson moved only from 0.879 to 0.856 but MMRV rose from 0.015 to 0.222. A hallucinated object in the gripper "could lead to overestimation". VLM judge accuracy is "often only 65–80%". Fidelity on out-of-distribution policy-rollout data is much worse (FVD 250.2 vs 98.5 in distribution). **[PARTIAL]**: training the generator on policy-induced data is a crude version of "be accurate where the policies go", but nothing is weighted by disagreement and no predictive score is proposed. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11520v3)
- **RoboWorld** (Jeon, Ye, Doo, Kim, Seo, Son, Lee; arXiv 2607.01060, July 2026) combines an autoregressive video world model, a VLM judge and "Step Forcing" to reduce the train-test mismatch. It reports Pearson r = 0.989 and Spearman ρ = 0.970 against real evaluation. An ICML 2026 listing with the same authors and numbers says this covers 8 policies and 4,188 rollouts against the RoboArena leaderboard. **[DIFFERENT]** — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01060); [ICML 2026 listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/76610)
- **1X World Model** (1X blog, 16 Jun 2025) finds "a correlation between the predicted success rates and the true task scores" but gives no coefficient. It states that "a World Model with 70% accuracy can accurately predict the better policy with 90% success" given a "true real-world success rate gap of 15%", without explaining the computation. It also says "Imitation learning objectives are typically not correlated with real world performance" and that the model struggles with held-out objects. **[DIFFERENT]**, but the link between gap size and reliability is relevant. — [1X page](https://1x.tech/discover/redwood-ai-world-model)
- **PlayWorld** (arXiv 2603.09030) reports Pearson 0.8766 between predicted and real success. This figure comes from a search snippet only and was not verified. **[DIFFERENT]** — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.09030)
- **Evidence that world-model rankings can fail badly:** AnyRank (Badithela et al., Princeton/UPenn/TRI; arXiv 2610.14603, Oct 2026) used a world-model prior whose correlation with real RoboArena rankings was only **r = 0.05**, against r = 0.89 for a DROID towel-folding prior. The fetched text does not say which world model was used. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14603)
- **Generative and perceptual metrics do not predict decision usefulness:**
  - World-in-World (arXiv 2510.18135, ICLR 2026 oral) finds "visual quality alone does not guarantee task success, and controllability matters more". **[DIFFERENT; supports the negative half of the headline]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.18135)
  - WorldArena (Shang et al., arXiv 2602.08971, Feb 2026) uses 16 perception metrics and functional tests that include *policy evaluator*. Across 14 models it reports a "significant perception-functionality gap": "high visual quality does not necessarily translate into strong embodied task capability". No correlation statistic is given in the abstract. **[PARTIAL: same negative claim, measured across many generators, but no decision-weighted replacement]** — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08971)
- **Sim2Real Predictivity** (Kadian et al., arXiv 1912.06321, IEEE RA-L 2020) introduced the Sim-vs-Real Correlation Coefficient (SRCC) over 9 navigation models. The default Habitat SRCC for success was **0.18**, because agents exploited simulator collision physics by sliding along walls. Tuning simulator parameters raised SRCC to **0.844**. **[PARTIAL]**: the earliest clear case of selecting simulator settings to maximise *ranking predictivity*. The failure mechanism is a simulator artefact that only some policies exploit, which is an error in the region where policies behave differently, though the paper does not frame it that way. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06321v2)
- **Decision-centric position paper** (Yu, Zhang, Sheng, Ren, Lin; Nanjing Univ.; arXiv 2606.15032, June 2026; v2 retitled "...for Embodied Decision-Making?"). It argues that "A model can generate plausible videos while misranking policies or misestimating success". It proposes an L0-L7 ladder and metrics including interventional error over task-relevant features, **action-effect agreement between two actions from the same history**, occupancy mismatch under the target policy's occupancy, pairwise ranking accuracy and MMRV, exploitability gap, calibration, and a gated composite. It notes most evaluators (e.g. WorldGym) test fixed-policy ranking only. It has **no explicit weighting of error by policy disagreement** and no experiments, and it does not cite VAML or value-equivalence. **[PARTIAL; closest conceptual neighbour in robotics]** — [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15032v1); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15032)
- A 2026 survey states that a reliable simulator should rank checkpoints as real execution does, and that a learned simulator can look convincing yet misrank. This comes from a search snippet only. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00113v1)

### Inferences
- The scattered evidence fits the headline's mechanism. Common-mode errors such as mass and friction offsets, or uniform under-estimation in Veo and Ctrl-World, leave rankings intact. Errors that affect policies differently, such as wall-sliding exploited by some agents, or the object axis where every policy fails and differences shrink below error, break them. **Nobody has turned this into a score.**
- MMRV should be the thesis's agreement metric, for comparability. A neat framing is that MMRV is decision-weighted on the **output** side (real outcome gaps), while the proposed score is decision-weighted on the **input/generator** side.
- The literature validates *one or two* world models against a few policies. The thesis's meta-evaluation across ~28 generator variants is unusual. Only WorldArena (14 models) and World-in-World compare many generators, and they test functional utility rather than ranking specifically.

### Gaps
- Exact WorldEval numbers conflict between secondary sources (0.942 vs 0.79 Pearson); the paper tables were not read.
- Which world model produced AnyRank's r = 0.05 prior was not established.
- No primary source was found for a "1X World Model Challenge" evaluation track that compares world-model rankings with real ones. Only the 1X blog was found.
- RoboArena itself (distributed real-world pairwise evaluation; believed to be Atreya et al. 2025, [arXiv 2506.18123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18123), prior knowledge) was not fetched.

## Q2. Does any work weight world-model or generator error by where the policies behave differently ("policy-conditioned" or decision-weighted fidelity)?

### Takeaway
**No paper found weights generator error by the difference in outcomes between policies, or uses such a score to predict whether generated scenarios rank policies as reality does.** Nearby ideas fall into four groups:
- *occupancy-weighted* error under one target policy (position paper, RepBM)
- *adversarial/worst-case policy* weighting for learning a simulator (Dann, Mansour, Mohri 2026)
- *value-aware model learning* for control (VAML, value equivalence, objective mismatch)
- *proximity weighting* of synthetic scenarios to the planner's likely behaviour (pseudo-simulation)

The difference between two policies' outcome functions as the weight, and generator-only error with exact dynamics, both look unoccupied.

### Cited Findings
- **Dann, Mansour, Mohri, "Theoretical Foundations and Effective Algorithms for Policy-Aware Simulator Learning"** (arXiv 2605.29032, May 2026; v3 Sep 2026). Simulators should be learned for "strategic robustness rather than predictive accuracy", framed as a minimax game between the model and an adversarial policy. They bound "the global policy-value gap by the local critic's loss" and show an Error-MDP duality in which the reward is the one-step critic error. Prediction error in "strategically important regions" falls by 1.5-2.2x on continuous control. **[PARTIAL]**: the closest "accurate only where it matters" theory. It differs because the weighting comes from a worst-case optimising policy (not the disagreement between a fixed set of candidates), the target is value gap or optimisation (not ranking), and the model learns dynamics (not exogenous inputs). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29032)
- **Decision-centric position paper** (arXiv 2606.15032) proposes occupancy mismatch under the policy's own occupancy and action-effect agreement, which compares two actions from the same history and so is fidelity of a *difference*. Neither is applied to differences between policies' outcome distributions, and nothing is validated empirically. **[PARTIAL]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15032v1)
- **Representation Balancing MDPs** (Liu, Gottesman et al., NeurIPS 2018, arXiv 1805.09044) derives a finite-sample bound on value error for learned MDP models and trains the model with a balanced representation tied to the evaluation policy. It is tested on synthetic benchmarks and an HIV treatment simulator. **[PARTIAL]**: model error is targeted at one evaluation policy's distribution, not at differences between policies. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.09044); [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/980ecd059122ce2e50136bda65c25e07-Abstract.html)
- **Decision-aware model learning lineage** (prior knowledge, not re-fetched; must be cited as conceptual ancestors). All share the idea that "the model need only be accurate where it changes value or decisions", for *control or planning*, not for *ranking a fixed policy set with a generator of exogenous inputs*. **[PARTIAL each]**
  - Farahmand et al., Value-Aware Model Learning, AISTATS 2017 — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v54/farahmand17a.html)
  - Grimm et al., Value Equivalence Principle, NeurIPS 2020 — [arXiv 2011.03506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03506)
  - Lambert et al., Objective Mismatch in MBRL, 2020 — [arXiv 2002.04523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04523)
  - Abachi et al., Policy-Aware Model Learning — [arXiv 2003.00030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00030)
  - Voelcker et al., Value-Gradient weighted Model loss (VaGraM), ICLR 2022 — [arXiv 2204.01464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01464)
- **Pseudo-simulation** (Cao, Hallgarten, ..., Pavone, Geiger, Chitta; arXiv 2506.04218, CoRL 2025) adds synthetic 3D-Gaussian-Splatting observations around real logs. A **proximity-based weighting gives more importance to synthetic observations closest to the vehicle's likely behaviour**. **[PARTIAL]**: synthetic scenarios are weighted by a planner-conditioned relevance, but by where *one* planner goes, not where planners disagree. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04218)
- **NVIDIA video-world-model evaluator**: adding policy rollouts to training raised Square's Pearson from 0.505 to 0.847. This is policy-conditioned *data coverage*, not weighting. **[PARTIAL]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11520v3)

- **Positioning:** the headline is not taken. The nearest prior art is (a) the decision-aware model learning lineage, which is the conceptual ancestor and *must* be cited, (b) the 2026 decision-centric position paper and (c) Dann, Mansour, Mohri 2026, both very recent and both must be cited. The thesis can stand apart in three ways:
  - weighting by the **outcome difference between candidate policies**, not by a single policy's occupancy or a worst-case adversary
  - a **pre-hoc scalar score validated across ~28 generator variants** to predict MMRV or Spearman agreement
  - **exact dynamics with only the exogenous input generated**, which makes attribution clean
- A simple "simulation lemma for policy differences" falls out of the headline. The ranking of a pair is correct whenever the generator's error in the *difference* of expected outcomes is smaller than the true difference. That error is the integral of (generated − real input density) against (outcome_A − outcome_B). This is my derivation, not found stated in these sources. A search returned only generic simulation-lemma tightness work ([arXiv 2406.16249](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16249v1)).

### Gaps
- Full texts of Dann, Mansour, Mohri 2026 and of the position paper (second half) were not read. Either may contain a pairwise or ranking corollary.
- Classical stochastic-simulation "ranking and selection" and common-random-numbers literature (comparing systems under shared input noise) was not searched. It may contain the cleanest prior statement that common-mode input error cancels in comparisons.

## Q3. Autonomous driving: do generative scenario models used to test planners check that generated scenarios rank planners as real logs do? What about rare-event testing?

### Takeaway
I found **no AD paper that checks whether generated scenarios rank planners the same way real logs do.** AD validates **evaluation proxies against closed-loop simulation**, and the Pearson/Spearman checks are between metrics (open-loop vs closed-loop), not between generated and real worlds. Generative scenario models are validated by **distributional realism**: WOSAC's NLL-based composite and scene-generation metrics. They are also judged by claims that planners find their scenarios harder. Rare-event work (D2RL, NADE) reweights toward safety-critical events with **importance sampling to stay unbiased**, for estimating *one* AV's crash rate.

### Cited Findings
- **NAVSIM** (arXiv 2406.15349, NeurIPS 2024 D&B): its PDM score correlates about 0.7-0.8 with nuPlan closed-loop scores, against 0.2-0.5 for traditional open-loop metrics, on both Spearman and Pearson. **[DIFFERENT]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.15349); [NeurIPS PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/32768f7faf1995026ef9821c696f3404-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf)
- **Pseudo-simulation** (arXiv 2506.04218, CoRL 2025) uses synthetic 3DGS observations that vary position, heading and speed, with proximity weighting. It reaches R² = 0.8 with closed-loop simulation, against 0.7 for the best open-loop approach. A search snippet of the CoRL PDF mentions "83 diverse planners" on nuPlan and 6x fewer environment interactions; this was not confirmed from the abstract. **[PARTIAL]** — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04218); [CoRL PDF](https://www.cvlibs.net/publications/Cao2025CORL.pdf)
- A 2026 study, "Do Open-Loop Metrics Predict Closed-Loop Driving? A Cross-Benchmark Correlation Study of NAVSIM and Bench2Drive", addresses proxy-to-closed-loop correlation. Only the title was retrieved. — [arXiv 2605.00066](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00066)

The abstract does not quantify ranking changes or validate against reality. **[DIFFERENT]** — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10403)

It reports that RL planners are more challenged in its generated environments, especially long and adversarial ones, and supports 500 m routes. There is **no ranking-agreement validation**. **[DIFFERENT]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22496); [CVPR](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Rowe_Scenario_Dreamer_Vectorized_Latent_Diffusion_for_Generating_Driving_Simulation_Environments_CVPR_2025_paper.html)
- Other generative scenario models in this family, likewise validated by realism or diversity metrics rather than planner-ranking agreement (prior knowledge, not re-fetched). **[DIFFERENT]**
  - SLEDGE (Chitta et al., ECCV 2024): [arXiv 2403.17933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17933)
  - SceneDiffuser (Jiang et al., NeurIPS 2024): [arXiv 2412.12129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12129)
  - TrafficGen (Feng et al., ICRA 2023): [arXiv 2210.06609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06609)
- **D2RL** (Feng et al., Nature 615:620-627, 2023): background agents trained with dense deep RL validate AV safety "in an accelerated mode, without loss of unbiasedness", running 10^3 to 10^5 times faster. Non-critical states are removed so training focuses on safety-critical ones. **[PARTIAL]**: tail-focused reweighting that stays unbiased is a direct precedent for the proposed "tail reweighting" fix, but the target is one AV's failure rate, not ranking. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05732-2); [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10460886)
- **NADE** (Feng et al., Nature Communications 12:748, 2021) applies importance sampling only to a few critical variables at critical moments and leaves the rest naturalistic. This avoids the curse of dimensionality while keeping the crash-rate estimator unbiased. **[PARTIAL]** — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-21007-8); [ROSAP](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/73491)
- Older rare-event AV testing via adaptive importance sampling: O'Kelly et al., "Scalable End-to-End Autonomous Vehicle Testing via Rare-event Simulation", NeurIPS 2018. Prior knowledge: [arXiv 1811.00145](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00145). **[DIFFERENT]**

### Inferences
- AD has built the *infrastructure* for the thesis's question (Spearman/Pearson of proxy vs reference across many planners: 83 in pseudo-simulation) but points it at **metric proxies**, not at **generative scenario fidelity vs reality**. A thesis paper can say plainly that the AD literature validates generators by realism metrics and validates evaluation metrics by ranking, but does not validate generators by ranking.
- D2RL and NADE show that reweighting toward the tail can stay unbiased (importance weights). The thesis's tail-reweighting fix should state whether it changes the *generator* (which biases the scenario distribution) or reweights *evaluation* (which, with likelihood ratios, need not).

### Gaps
- No AD paper was found that runs the same planners on (a) real held-out logs and (b) generator-produced scenarios and compares rankings. A targeted check of the Scenario Dreamer, SLEDGE, SceneDiffuser and SMART-style papers' appendices could still turn one up.
- Whether CLOVER ([arXiv 2605.15120](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15120), "Closed-Loop Value Estimation and Ranking for End-to-End AD Planning") is relevant could not be determined; only the title was retrieved.

## Q4. Model-based OPE and simulator validity for ranking in other domains (healthcare, recommenders, building and grid control): any result on when a biased simulator still ranks policies correctly?

### Takeaway
OPE has standard **ranking metrics** (rank correlation, regret@k) and recent **model-selection-for-OPE** theory. Recommender research validates user simulators by **Spearman over historical A/B tests**. Building control validates synthetic weather and forecast-error generators by **distributional similarity only**, with no controller-ranking check found. **No general theorem was found for when a biased simulator still ranks policies correctly.** The empirical regularities point the same way across domains: common-mode bias is harmless, while differential or exploited artefacts and compressed gaps are harmful.

### Cited Findings
- **Model Selection for Off-policy Evaluation** (Liu, Zhao, Agarwal, Liu, Huang, Amortila, Jiang; NeurIPS 2025; arXiv 2502.08021) selects among candidate value functions or **candidate dynamics models** to evaluate a target policy, with guarantees and a new experimental protocol (Gym-Hopper). **[PARTIAL]**: analogous to selecting a generator, but by value accuracy for one policy. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08021v3); [NeurIPS](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/3d4dc72d715bd6415d356293079adf3d-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **Benchmarks for Deep Off-Policy Evaluation** (Fu et al., ICLR 2021) evaluates OPE by absolute error, **rank correlation** and **regret@k**. Prior knowledge: [arXiv 2103.16596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16596). **[DIFFERENT]**
- **AnyRank** (arXiv 2610.14603): "any inaccuracies in prior policy rankings do not invalidate the statistical assurances". It needs up to about 50% fewer hardware trials than Bonferroni on RoboArena and TRI-LBM. Effort shifts toward "hard-to-separate policies", indirectly through risk reallocation. Bradley-Terry baselines were miscalibrated. **[DIFFERENT]**: ranking uses real trials, and world models serve only as priors. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14603)
- **Recommender simulators:**
  - ContextSim (arXiv 2604.09549, 2026) measures Spearman between simulated and real engagement over **55 historical A/B tests** on a food platform and reports the highest correlation among baselines.
  - AlignUSER (arXiv 2601.00930) uses the same 55-test protocol (proprietary data).
  - **[DIFFERENT]** — [ContextSim](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.09549); [AlignUSER](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.00930)
- A 2026 sim-to-real study of persona-based ad-copy simulation keeps only real A/B tests with a **statistically reliable winner**, then compares within-package rankings. A no-persona baseline beat persona simulation. Filtering to resolvable pairs is a cousin of MMRV's gap weighting. **[DIFFERENT]** — [arXiv 2609.25010](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25010)
- Google/YouTube Music proposes user simulation to reduce live experiments for preference-elicitation policies and claims to predict live performance. Quantitative evidence was not retrieved. — [arXiv 2409.17436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17436v1)
- **Building control:** an LBNL study (Zheng, Zabala, Febres, Blum, Wang; J. Building Performance Simulation, 2025) models forecast error for temperature and solar with AR(1). It validates by low Jensen-Shannon divergence to real error distributions, is motivated by comparing advanced controllers, and is being added to BOPTEST. It does **not** check controller rankings. **[DIFFERENT]** — [LBNL](https://buildings.lbl.gov/publications/quantifying-and-simulating-weather)
- Sandia's MEWS stochastic heat-wave generator (with an EnergyPlus demonstration) reports load and comfort impacts, not strategy rankings. An older AIVC comparison of synthetic vs recorded weather across 50 cities compared annual energy and peak loads, not decision rankings. — [Sandia](https://www.sandia.gov/research/publications/details/multi-scenario-extreme-weather-simulator-application-to-heat-waves-2022-01-01/); [AIVC](https://aivc.org/taxonomy/term/151208)
- **When does a biased simulator still rank correctly? Empirical evidence:**
  - SIMPLER: mass and friction errors shift success by up to about 15% while MMRV stays low. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941v1)
  - Veo and Ctrl-World: rankings are mostly preserved despite systematic under-estimation. — [Veo](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10675v2); [Ctrl-World](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10125v3)
  - Veo: low success for every policy on the object axis makes ranking unreliable. — [Veo](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10675v2)
  - Kadian: an artefact exploited only by some agents drops SRCC to 0.18. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06321v2)
  - 1X: reliability depends on the true gap (15% gap, 70% accuracy, 90% correct pick). — [1X](https://1x.tech/discover/redwood-ai-world-model)
- Theory found is only adjacent:
  - simulation-lemma tightness under misspecification ([arXiv 2406.16249](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16249v1))
  - multiple-comparison error inflation when choosing among many policies from simulation bounds ([arXiv 2309.10874](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.10874); snippet only)
  - recalibration under drift with a misspecified simulator (Parikh et al., [arXiv 2605.21458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21458), which does not address ranking)

### Inferences
- Across robotics, recommenders and OPE, the community's answer to "is the simulator good enough?" is **post-hoc**: run real trials and correlate. The thesis's contribution would be a **pre-hoc predictor** (decision-weighted fidelity computed from inputs plus policy outcome differences) that forecasts that correlation. That appears new.
- Building and grid control with synthetic weather is the **structural twin** of the thesis: an exogenous driver feeds an exact physics model. Yet the controller-ranking validity of synthetic weather appears unstudied there, which makes it a natural second domain for generality claims.

### Gaps
- No healthcare-simulator ranking-validity study was found in this pass (e.g. sepsis or HIV simulators used for OPE), and none was searched in depth.
- No grid-control study comparing controller rankings under synthetic vs historical load or renewables was found.

## Q5. Does any work separate generator (exogenous-input) error from dynamics error when generated-world rankings fail?

### Takeaway
**None found.** Robotics world models generate observations *and* dynamics jointly, so ranking errors are attributed qualitatively (physics, hallucination, retries) without decomposition. The partial decompositions are in physics simulators (SIMPLER's control gap vs visual gap) or in modular AD pipelines (scene generator vs behaviour model; sensor synthesis vs non-reactive agents), and none of them is tied to ranking failures of a *generator*. The thesis setup, exact SWMM dynamics with only rain generated, is a clean identification design with no counterpart found in robotics or AD.

### Cited Findings
- SIMPLER separates the **control gap** (system identification; MMRV 0.031 vs 0.070/0.100 under perturbed gains) from the **visual gap** (green-screen and texture matching; MMRV 0.050) by ablation. It does not rank the two against each other. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941v1)
- Ctrl-World and the NVIDIA evaluator list error sources (contact physics, missing retries, under-represented failures, hallucination, autoregressive drift, multi-view inconsistency, 65-80% VLM judge accuracy) but **do not attribute ranking errors to specific causes**. — [Ctrl-World](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10125v3); [NVIDIA](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11520v3)
- The Veo evaluator mixes three error sources: the generative scene edits (image editing, multi-view completion), the action-conditioned video dynamics, and human scoring. No decomposition is reported. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10675v2)
- Scenario Dreamer architecturally separates initial-scene generation (latent diffusion) from behaviour simulation (autoregressive transformer) but does not validate planner rankings against real data. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22496)
- Pseudo-simulation keeps real logs and synthesises only sensor observations around them, approximating "generate the input, keep the world". It is validated against closed-loop *simulation*, not reality. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04218)
- The 2026 position paper's ladder treats counterfactual action fidelity and closed-loop validity as separate levels. This is a conceptual separation of one-step dynamics fidelity from rollout validity, not of exogenous-input error from dynamics error. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15032v1)

### Inferences
- The thesis can claim a **methodological contribution** beyond the score: an "exact-dynamics, generated-exogenous-input" testbed in which any wrong verdict is by construction the generator's fault. Robotics and AD world-model evaluators cannot isolate this, because their learned models entangle input and dynamics.
- For the paper's related-work framing, a reasonable contrast is: "robotics and AD ask *whether* generated-world rankings match reality; we ask *which generator errors* cause mismatches, and answer it in a setting where generator error is the only error".

### Gaps
- I did not find hydrology, building or grid studies that use the same exact-simulator design to test policy-ranking fidelity of synthetic exogenous inputs. Other researchers covering hydrology and energy may find such work.
